Putting your lifts on a profile is optional, and the right answer depends on why you'd add them. Include them if they're honest and they help someone understand what training means to you. Leave them out if they'd be the only thing that defines you. SetsOfLove lets you enter your bench press, squat, deadlift and overhead press, or skip the lot.

## What does SetsOfLove show?

The lifts section is optional. If you fill it in, you can enter your numbers for the bench press, squat, deadlift and overhead press in kg or lb. SetsOfLove shows each one in both units, with your chosen unit first. A lift entered as 100 kg appears as 100 kg (220 lb).

Everything is self-reported. Nobody checks your numbers, which is the main reason honesty matters. A made-up number only creates an awkward moment at a gym later. If you're not sure what to enter, [how to track your lifts](/blog/how-to-track-your-lifts/) explains how to keep a reliable record.

## The case for adding them

- **They're concrete.** If you like numbers and straight talk, a lift is a plain fact. It's more informative than "I'm into fitness".
- **They show commitment, not appearance.** Numbers say how long you've been at it without anyone commenting on bodies.
- **They start conversations.** Someone who lifts can ask how you set up your squat. Someone who doesn't can ask what a deadlift even is.
- **They attract your kind of person.** A reader who also lifts may read the section and immediately feel at home.

## The case for leaving them out

- **A number is a small slice of you.** It tells a reader nothing about how you treat people.
- **It invites comparison.** Some readers will rank themselves against it, and that's rarely a good start.
- **It can age fast.** If you're coming back from a break or an injury, your numbers may change. Any edit sends a live profile back for approval, so a stale figure isn't something you'd want to fix often.
- **It may not suit what you're after.** If you're looking for friendship or a gentle first date, a list of maxes may be beside the point.

None of this is a reason to skip them. It's a reason to be deliberate.

## A decision rule

Lifts are only one way to say what training means to you. You could say the same thing in words, such as how often you train and what you enjoy about it.

Ask 3 questions.

1. Would I be comfortable if someone read these numbers and then watched me lift? If not, they're not ready for a profile.
2. Do the numbers say something about me that my other fields don't? If yes, add them.
3. Am I adding them to be liked, or to be accurate? Only the second is a good reason.

If you answer yes, yes and accurate, add them. Otherwise, leave the section blank and put the energy into your "about you" text.

## Being honest about the numbers

Use real figures from a recent session. A clean single rep you did 6 months ago isn't the same as what you'd do today. Pick one standard and stick to it, such as your best set of one rep with good form.

If you're newer to lifting, it's fine to share small numbers. They show where you are, and everyone started somewhere. If you have a coach or a physio, ask them what's sensible to aim for, and never chase a number on a profile at the cost of how you feel. Training is meant to leave you better off, and a profile figure isn't worth a sore back.

## How to word it around the numbers

The numbers can sit beside a sentence in your text fields. For example:

- "Slowly building a bench I'm proud of."
- "Deadlifts are my favourite day of the week."
- "I track my training, but I'm more interested in whether you like a long walk."

A line like this changes a figure from a flex into a personality. It also keeps the focus on how you talk about training, which says more than a number does.

If you convert between units yourself, [kg to lb: converting your lifts](/blog/kg-to-lb-converting-your-lifts/) has the formula and a table. And for readers who are new to these lifts, [the big four lifts explained for beginners](/blog/the-big-four-lifts-explained/) explains what each one trains.

## So, should you?

There's no wrong answer, only a more or less comfortable one.

Add them if they're honest, recent and true to how you'd talk about training in person. Leave them out if they'd make you uneasy, or if they'd crowd out the rest of you. You can always start without them. Once your profile is approved, you can add them later, knowing the profile goes offline until it's approved again.
