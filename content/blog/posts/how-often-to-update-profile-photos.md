---
title: How often should you update your profile photos?
description: How often to update dating profile photos: refresh when you change, not on a timer, and batch edits because each one needs approval again.
---
Update your dating profile photos when you look noticeably different, not on a fixed timer. For most people that means a refresh every 6 to 12 months, plus any time something big changes. On SetsOfLove, every edit sends your profile for approval again, so batch your changes into one go.

## What should drive the timing?

The real test is simple: would someone recognise you from your photos at a café? If yes, your photos are fine. If they'd hesitate, it's time.

That test beats a calendar. A photo from 2 years ago may still look exactly like you. A photo from 3 months ago may not, if you've cut or grown your hair, changed glasses or started wearing something different.

Here are the usual triggers:

- A big change in hair, facial hair or glasses.
- A change in how you look from training, weight or life stage, in either direction.
- A new job, city or look that you'd like the profile to reflect.
- A photo that no longer feels like you.
- A new season, if your photos show a very different setting from now.

Notice that none of these ask you to look a certain way. The goal is to look like yourself today.

## How recent is recent enough?

A decent rule of thumb is to keep your main photo within about the last year, and the rest within about 2. That isn't a rule of the app. It's a sensible guide.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Looks the same as your photos | Leave them |
| Small changes, such as a haircut | Replace the main photo only |
| Big change, such as a new look or style | Replace most or all photos |
| Seasonal shift, such as summer to winter | Optional, add one photo that fits |
| 12 months or more since your last refresh | Check, then update if needed |

Recent photos matter because they set honest expectations. If you meet someone and they can't connect your photos to your face, the first minute is awkward before anything has begun. Clear, current photos spare you both.

## Why batch your changes?

This is the SetsOfLove-specific part. Every profile is approved by the admin before it goes live. Any edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again, and that includes a single photo swap.

So if you change one photo on Monday and another on Thursday, your profile goes offline twice. Anyone who opens your link in the gaps may not see your profile at all. We don't quote how long approval takes, so plan as if it could take a while.

Batching is the fix:

1. Keep a running note of photos you want to swap.
2. Wait until you have 2 or 3 changes ready.
3. Make all of them in one sitting.
4. Send the profile for approval once.
5. Avoid editing right before you share your link or at the start of a busy week.

Think of it like planning a deload week: you change a few things together, then leave them alone while they settle.

## What makes a good replacement photo?

Don't just swap for the sake of it. Replace a photo only if the new one is better, not merely newer.

- It's sharp, well lit and clearly you.
- Your face is visible in the main photo, without a cap or sunglasses shading it.
- You're the main person, and nobody else is identifiable in the background.
- It has no text, logos, memes or screenshots.
- It shows something different from your other photos.

## Should you change the written parts at the same time?

Often, yes. If you're going offline for a refresh anyway, it's the perfect time to reread your bio, your lifts if you've entered them, and your "who you'd like to meet" text. A quick tidy while you're in there saves a second round later.

Check these while you're editing:

- Do your lifts reflect your current working numbers? They're optional and self-reported, so keep them honest.
- Does your bio still describe your week?
- Does your "what you're after" selection still feel true?

If a rule slips through, a rejection comes with a note on what to change. [How profile approval works on SetsOfLove](/blog/how-profile-approval-works/) walks through it.

## A simple schedule you can actually keep

You don't need to think about this often. Try this:

- Put a reminder in your calendar twice a year, perhaps at the start of summer and winter.
- When it pops up, open your profile and ask the café test: would someone recognise me?
- If yes, do nothing and close the tab.
- If no, collect your changes and send them for approval in one go.
- Between reminders, if something big changes, treat that as an extra trigger.

That's the whole system. It costs about 5 minutes twice a year, it keeps your profile honest, and it keeps your link live as much as possible. That's about the right amount of effort.
