The best places to meet active people beyond the weights floor are the ones where you do something together on a regular schedule: park runs, run clubs, climbing gyms, community sport, charity events and volunteering. Pick 1 that suits your pace and budget, go several weeks in a row, and let friendships form at their own speed.

## Where are the best places to meet active people?

A gym is a good place to meet people, but it isn't the only one. Plenty of people use headphones, train alone or just want to be left in peace. Outside the gym, the activity is shared and the social part is built in. Someone showing up to a group run expects to say hello.

It also takes pressure off. If a friendship or something more develops, it comes from doing things together, not from approaching a stranger mid-session. And if you're new to a city, as many people are, it widens your circle quickly.

Whatever you choose, the same rules apply. Be friendly, keep early chats short, respect anyone who'd rather not talk, and accept a quiet reply. If you want a hand with the basics, [how to make friends at the gym as an adult](/blog/how-to-make-friends-at-the-gym/) works just as well for other settings.

## Park runs and run clubs

[parkrun](https://www.parkrun.com.au/) holds free, weekly, timed 5 km events, mostly on Saturday mornings, and walkers are welcome as well as runners. Volunteers run each event, so you can also help out without running at all. There's usually a coffee afterwards, which is where much of the chatting happens.

Run clubs are similar but more local. Many meet at a set time and place each week, and some have a few pace groups so you're not left behind. Look for a club that welcomes beginners if you're new to running.

Good for: people who like structure, mornings and a relaxed social side.
Watch for: the pace. Ask about walking or slower groups first.

## Climbing gyms

Indoor climbing has a naturally social feel. People take turns on routes, share tips and cheer each other on. You also spend time standing around waiting, which makes chatting easy.

Many climbing gyms run intro sessions, and some run social nights. If you're new, a beginner class is a gentle way in. The staff will teach you the basics and safety, which you should follow carefully.

Good for: people who like problem-solving and short bursts of activity.
Watch for: cost. Check prices and what's included before you commit.

## Community sport

Social leagues in sports such as netball, touch football, volleyball, soccer, cricket or basketball often welcome beginners. They tend to meet weekly, and the games are followed by a chat.

Look for words like "social", "beginners" or "mixed". You can often join as an individual and be placed in a team.

Good for: people who like team activities and being part of a regular group.
Watch for: the commitment. A season runs for weeks, so check the dates.

## Charity events and volunteering

Charity walks, fun runs and fundraising events attract friendly, goal-oriented people. Volunteering, whether at a community sporting event, a trail clean-up or a local program, puts you beside people who share your values.

Volunteering is also a low-pressure way to chat, because there's always a task to focus on. If conversation dries up, you can simply get back to work.

Good for: people who prefer purpose over performance.
Watch for: reliability. Show up when you say you will, because organisers count on volunteers.

## How to choose one

Use this table to narrow it down.

| Option | Pace | Social style | Best if you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Park runs | Walk, jog or run | Light chat, coffee after | Want something free and regular |
| Run clubs | Varies by group | Group chat, often a post-run catch-up | Like a routine and pace groups |
| Climbing gyms | Self-paced | Taking turns, tips | Enjoy variety and learning |
| Community sport | Team pace | Team chat, game nights | Like teamwork |
| Charity events | Event days | Big crowds, shared goals | Prefer a cause |
| Volunteering | Task-based | Working side by side | Like a purpose |

A decision rule helps: choose the one you'd still turn up to if you made no friends at all. If you enjoy the activity itself, the people will follow.

## Make it stick

Most friendships come from repeat contact. A simple plan:

1. **Pick 1 option** and go 4 weeks in a row.
2. **Arrive a few minutes early** and stay a few minutes after.
3. **Learn 2 names** a month. That's enough.
4. **Say yes** to a coffee, a group photo or a catch-up, if you want to.
5. **Review after a month.** Keep it, or try something else.

If you also want the friendships to lead somewhere, take the same slow approach as at the gym. Ask people about the activity, not their personal life. If you're considering asking someone out, [is it okay to ask someone out at the gym?](/blog/is-it-okay-to-ask-someone-out-at-the-gym/) covers principles that work anywhere: build a friendly base first, ask plainly and accept a no.

Finally, there's another low-pressure route. If you and a friend both train, you can each make a SetsOfLove profile and share your link with people you trust. There are no messages or likes, so no one gets pressured, and introductions happen through friends or in person. But it's only one option among many, and a Saturday morning in the park works fine too.
