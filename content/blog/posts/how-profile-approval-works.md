Dating profile approval on SetsOfLove is simple: the admin approves every profile before it goes live. If you edit a live profile, it goes offline until it's approved again. If a profile is rejected or taken down, you get a note saying what to change.

## What does approval mean?

Nothing on SetsOfLove goes public automatically. When you finish your profile and send it for approval, the admin looks it over. Once it's approved, it lives at its own link, like setsoflove.com/sam-t, and you can share it.

The point is to keep the space friendly and clean for everyone, including you. Anyone who opens your link has seen a profile that passed the same checks as every other one.

We're not going to quote a review time here, because that wouldn't be honest. Plan for it taking some time, and don't schedule your first share for the moment you hit send.

## What is checked?

The admin looks at what the rules already say, so you can check your own profile first. Here is a plain list.

**Photos**

- You have 1 to 4 photos.
- Your main photo shows your face clearly.
- Gym and workout photos are welcome.
- No nudity and nothing sexual.
- No photos of other people without their consent.
- No screenshots, memes or images with text or logos.

**Written fields**

- Contact details such as phone numbers and social handles aren't allowed in public text fields.
- Your link uses lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, 3 to 30 characters.

If you want the full detail on photos, [photo rules: what's welcome and what isn't](/blog/setsoflove-photo-rules/) goes through each one. The tappable quick picks under most fields are there to help you get started, and you can edit anything they add.

## What happens if a profile is rejected?

You get a note on what to change. That's the whole idea: a rejection isn't a verdict on you, it's a to-do list. Typically that means swapping a photo, trimming a line or removing a detail.

The same goes for a takedown. If a live profile is taken offline, the note explains what to fix so you can change it and send it again.

A sensible way to respond:

1. Read the note once, slowly.
2. Fix exactly what it names, and nothing else.
3. Check the rest of the profile against the lists above while you're there.
4. Send it for approval again.

Fixing only what's named keeps your next round short. It also means you won't accidentally add a new problem while editing something unrelated.

## Why does an edit take a live profile offline?

Because approval applies to what's on the page. If you change a photo or a line, the admin hasn't seen the new version yet, so the profile goes offline until it's approved again.

That has a practical consequence. Anyone who opens your link while you're waiting won't see your profile. If you've just shared it with a friend, they won't see your profile until it's approved again.

So change things in batches, not in a trickle:

- Decide everything you want to change first.
- Make all the edits in one sitting.
- Avoid editing the night before you share your link with someone, or in the middle of a conversation about it.

If you're thinking about swapping photos, [how often should you update your profile photos](/blog/how-often-to-update-profile-photos/) has a simple rule for deciding when it's worth it.

## How can you get approved the first time?

Most of it is checking your own profile before you send it. Run through this list:

- Is your face clear in the main photo, without a cap or sunglasses hiding it?
- Are you the main person in every photo, with nobody else identifiable in the background?
- Is there any text, logo or screenshot in an image?
- Have you left any phone number or handle in a written field?
- Is the profile written in your own words, in a friendly tone?

For common slip-ups to avoid, see [9 profile mistakes gym-goers make](/blog/profile-mistakes-gym-goers-make/), and for the written side, [what not to put on a dating profile](/blog/what-not-to-put-on-your-profile/).

## Does approval mean someone is verified?

No. Approval means a profile passed a review against the rules. It doesn't confirm who anyone is. Gym membership isn't checked and lifts are self-reported, so approval isn't proof of any of that.

That's why you should keep your usual habits when you meet someone, whoever introduced you. And every public profile has a "Report this profile" link if something looks wrong.

Approval is a gate, not a promise. It keeps the page tidy and the standards clear, and it asks you to do a small amount of work once. After that, your profile is yours to share, on your terms.
