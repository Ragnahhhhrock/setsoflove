Sharing your profile link is reasonably safe if you share it deliberately. A SetsOfLove profile is a page that anyone with the link can open, so the safest approach is to put the link only where you'd be comfortable with the people there seeing it. Know what the page shows, choose your audience, and you stay in control.

## What a SetsOfLove profile shows

Each approved profile lives at its own link, such as setsoflove.com/sam-t. Anyone who has that link can open the page. It's helpful to know exactly what's on it, because that tells you what you're sharing.

A profile can include:

- 1 to 4 photos, with a main photo that shows your face clearly.
- First name, age and gender.
- Who you'd like to meet and what you're after.
- Suburb, city and country.
- What you do and how you train.
- A short "about you" and a few words on who you'd like to meet.
- Optional lifts, if you choose to enter them.

Think of it as the details you'd tell someone at the gym over a few weeks. Check the suburb and city fields in particular, and decide whether that level of detail suits you.

## What a profile doesn't show

Equally useful is what's absent. Contact details such as phone numbers and social handles aren't allowed in public text fields. There's no messaging, no comments and no likes, so nobody can send you a note through the page. Search engines are told not to list profiles, so a stranger searching for your name shouldn't find the page that way.

That last point deserves a careful reading. It reduces how easily the page turns up in search results, but it doesn't make the page private. Once you've shared a link, the person who received it can forward it, and a link in a group chat travels as far as the group does. Treat the link as something you hand out, not something that stays where you put it.

## Who is safe to share your profile link with?

The question to ask is: who will see this, and could they pass it on? A few rough tiers:

- **Close friends you trust.** Low risk, and the friends can introduce you.
- **A training group chat you know well.** Reasonable, if you know most people in it.
- **A large public group or an open social post.** The widest reach, so it's the one to think hardest about.

A simple decision rule: don't post it anywhere you wouldn't post your photos. If you want a hand with wording, our post on [how to share your profile link without it being awkward](/blog/how-to-share-your-profile-link/) has ideas for what to say and where.

## Check what you've put on the page

The best way to keep a shared link safe is to keep the content safe. Before you send a profile for approval, go through each field and ask whether it gives away more than you'd tell a new acquaintance.

- Keep your gym's name, your exact training times and your workplace details out of your text.
- Use a link name that doesn't give away your full surname. Link names are lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, 3 to 30 characters long.
- Make sure no photo shows your street, your car's number plate or other people's faces. Photos of other people need their consent.

Our guides to [what not to put on a dating profile](/blog/what-not-to-put-on-your-profile/) and [a privacy checklist for any dating profile](/blog/a-privacy-checklist-for-any-dating-profile/) go through these in more detail.

## Remember that a profile isn't verified

Every profile is approved by the admin before it goes live, but gym membership isn't checked and lifts are self-reported. Approval is a check against the profile rules, not a guarantee about the person. If you receive a link from someone and want to meet them, follow the usual habits: a video call first, a public place, a friend who knows your plans. The [online dating safety tips for Australians](/blog/online-dating-safety-tips-for-australians/) cover these.

If you ever receive a link that seems to use someone else's photos, or a profile that breaks the rules, each public profile has a "Report this profile" link.

## If you want to pull your link back

You can't recall a link that's already been shared, but you can change what it leads to.

- **Edit your profile.** Any edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again. If you want the page out of view quickly, a small edit does that. You can then decide whether to resubmit.
- **Change what's on it.** If a detail is more specific than you now want, edit it before it goes back up.
- **Ask for help.** If you want your profile removed altogether, the contact address is the place to ask.

Beyond SetsOfLove, remember that anyone who saw the page could have taken a screenshot. That's one more reason to keep the content to what you're comfortable having seen.

If you're ever harassed as a result of something shared online, the [eSafety Commissioner](https://www.esafety.gov.au/) provides information and ways to report. If you feel in danger, call 000.

## A short summary

Share the link with people you trust, expect it to travel, and keep the page itself to details you're comfortable with. That's the whole approach. Members choose where their link goes, and nothing on SetsOfLove pushes it anywhere for them.
