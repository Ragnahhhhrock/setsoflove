title: Dating over 40 when you train
description: Dating over 40 when you train comes down to honesty, shared values and treating people as individuals. Practical ways to show up as yourself.
---
Dating over 40 when you train works best when you lead with who you are now, not who you were at 25. Be honest about your age, your goals and your life, and look for shared values over shared stats. Your training habits show discipline and consistency, but they're not a personality.

## What you bring that you didn't have at 25

By your 40s, you've probably learned what you can and can't compromise on. You know how you handle stress, how you like to spend a weekend and what you've learned from past relationships. That clarity is useful.

Training adds to it. Showing up on cold mornings and sticking with a plan are real strengths in a partnership. So is knowing how to rest.

Don't oversell any of this. You don't need to present yourself as a finished product. Someone who is curious, kind and direct is easy to like at any age.

## Be plain about your age

Put your real age on your profile and use recent photos. Few things damage trust faster than someone turning up looking noticeably different from their pictures. It's also a fair test of your own comfort. If you're not comfortable being seen as you are today, that's worth working on before you meet anyone.

Here are some practical points:

- Use photos from the last 12 months. If you've changed a lot since an older photo, leave it out.
- Make sure the main photo shows your face clearly, with no cap or sunglasses. [Why your main photo should show your face](/blog/main-photo-show-your-face/) explains why this matters.
- Add one photo that shows something you do outside the gym, like a walk or a meal with friends.

## Say what you're after

At this stage, people often have clear ideas, and they often have different ones. One person wants a partner to share a home with. Another wants company and independence. Someone else wants to be friends first.

Work out your answer, then say it plainly. If you're not sure how, [what to say about what you're after](/blog/what-are-you-looking-for/) walks through it step by step.

Say it kindly and early. "I'd like something long term but I'm not in a hurry" is clear without being heavy.

## Look for values, not statistics

It's tempting to use numbers as shorthand: lifts, age gaps, how many sessions a week. These tell you very little about whether you'll get along.

Better questions to hold in mind:

- How do they treat people who can do nothing for them, like a waiter or a new member at the gym?
- Do they stay curious about things they don't know?
- Are they at ease with their own routine and with yours?
- How do they talk about their past relationships?

A person who trains 3 days a week and one who trains 6 can suit each other very well. A shared sense of humour, similar views on family, money and time, and mutual respect do far more work.

## Avoid age-based assumptions

Assumptions about age cut both ways. Don't assume someone in their 40s is jaded, set in their ways or only after a particular thing. Don't assume someone younger is not serious. Don't assume that someone older can't keep up, or that someone fit must be vain.

Treat each profile as one person's words about themselves. Read it all. Notice what they've chosen to say and what they've asked for. If it says "friendship and dates", take that at face value.

If you're returning to dating after a long time, you might find the whole process unfamiliar. [Dating again after a long relationship](/blog/dating-again-after-a-long-relationship/) covers that part.

## Training with an older body

Many people start lifting, running or doing classes in their 40s or later. Others have trained for decades and are managing old niggles. Neither is more worthy.

If you have an injury, a health condition or a concern about getting back to training, talk to a doctor or physio. It's no one else's business until you decide to share it, and there's no need to apologise for it. When it comes up on a date, you can say it simply: "I'm working around a shoulder, so I'm doing lighter work for now."

Don't turn your body or your training into a topic of comparison. Skip remarks about what someone should be lifting or eating. Nobody asked, and nobody enjoys it.

## Treat profiles, and people, with respect

Whatever age you are, a few habits make you a better person to meet.

1. **Read before you reach out.** If a profile says what someone's after, take it seriously.
2. **Accept an answer.** A no, or no reply at all, is an answer. See [how to handle rejection gracefully](/blog/how-to-handle-rejection-gracefully/) if you want a plan for it.
3. **Keep personal details private early on.** Share a first name and a suburb, not your home address or your daily timetable.
4. **Meet in a public place** and let a friend know where you're going.

You don't need to be younger, fitter or more impressive than you are. You need to be honest, and willing to be curious.
