description: Not sure what to write about yourself on a dating profile? Try these prompts, 3 simple structures and weak-versus-strong rewrites.
---
If you're not sure what to write about yourself on a dating profile, answer one concrete question instead: what does a normal week look like for you? Then add one thing you're into outside training and one thing you're looking for. 3 honest sentences beat a long paragraph of adjectives.

## Where to start when the box is empty

Start with the quick picks. On SetsOfLove, tappable suggestions sit under most fields, and tapping one adds it to your text. Tap it again and it comes out. They're plain, positive starting points, not finished answers.

Pick 2 or 3 that are true for you. Then edit them until they sound like you. You can change anything. A quick pick like "Early morning weights" can become "Early morning weights, then a very large breakfast", which tells a reader something the pick didn't.

## 3 structures you can borrow

If a blank page is the problem, a frame helps. Fill in the gaps with your own details.

1. **Now, then, next.** What you do now, what you used to do, what you're heading toward. "I train 4 mornings a week. I used to run, and I still do on Sundays. Next up is a first 10 km race."
2. **2 facts and a plan.** 2 things that are true, one thing you'd like to do. "I work in logistics and I'm a terrible cook. I'd like to learn to make one good curry."
3. **A week in a line.** "Weights on Monday, Wednesday and Friday, markets on Saturday, a long lazy lunch on Sunday."

Each one is 3 sentences. Each one gives a reader something to ask about. That matters more than it sounds, because a profile with no hooks leaves a reader nothing to say. A detail like a first pull-up or a half-finished bookshelf gives a friend of a friend an easy first question if you ever meet.

## Weak lines and stronger rewrites

The trick is to swap a general claim for a small, real detail. Here are 5 examples.

| Weak | Stronger | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| I love fitness. | I train 4 mornings a week and I'm slowly getting my first pull-up. | A number and a goal |
| I'm easy going. | I'm happy with a walk or a coffee, and I'll be early. | Shows it, doesn't claim it |
| Looking for someone genuine. | I'd like to meet someone who says what they mean, kindly. | Positive and specific |
| I like to travel. | I've done Bali twice and I'm saving for Tasmania. | Names the places |
| No drama. | I like plans that are simple and communicated early. | Says what you want |

Notice what changed. The weak lines would fit anyone, and the strong ones couldn't. Notice also that none of the stronger lines is a boast.

## Prompts to unstick yourself

Pick one, write a sentence, move on. You don't need all of them.

- What's the first thing you do after a session?
- What do you cook when you can't be bothered?
- What's the last thing that made you laugh at the gym?
- What's a place you'd happily return to?
- What do you do on a rest day?
- What would a friend say you're oddly passionate about?
- What does a good Sunday look like?

A good answer is usually one sentence, and it's fine to be a little funny. One light line is plenty, and it should never be at someone else's expense. If you write 3 and love all of them, you've got your section.

## Checks on length and tone

Aim for 3 to 5 sentences. Short enough to read in 20 seconds, long enough to have a personality.

Keep the framing positive. Say what you like and what you'd like to find. Avoid lists of what you won't tolerate, because they read as a warning sign instead of an invitation.

Skip contact details, social handles and phone numbers. They're not allowed in public text fields on SetsOfLove, and for good reason. [What not to put on a dating profile](/blog/what-not-to-put-on-your-profile/) covers the other things to keep private, such as your exact routine.

## Putting it together

Here's a 3-sentence version built from the structures above:

"I train 4 mornings a week and I'm slowly working towards my first pull-up. Weekends are for markets, long lunches and a half-finished bookshelf. I'd like to meet someone who is easy to talk to and happy with a slow Saturday."

It took 3 prompts and about 10 minutes. Read yours out loud once. If it sounds like you, it's done.
