Your dating profile main photo should show your face clearly, because it's how someone recognises that the profile is really you. No sunglasses or hat should shade it, and you should be the person in it. On SetsOfLove this is a rule for the main photo, and the other 3 photos can show whatever else you like within the photo rules.

## What does "clearly shows your face" mean?

It means someone could look at the photo and see you. Your eyes are visible, your face is in good light and nothing is blocking it. The photo is in focus and recent enough that you'd still look like it in person.

It doesn't mean a particular look. You don't need makeup, a smile you don't feel, a certain haircut or a studio. It doesn't need to fit anyone's idea of how a person should look. Present as yourself. A relaxed face in daylight does the job better than anything staged.

## Why does it matter?

There are 3 practical reasons.

1. **Recognition.** On SetsOfLove, introductions happen through friends or at the gym. If a friend sends someone your link, or you walk past each other at your gym, they should be able to tell it's you. A profile that can't be linked to a real face is a puzzle.
2. **Trust.** People are wary online, and with good reason. A clear face tells a reader there's a real person behind the profile. It's one small signal in a set of habits that make a profile feel honest.
3. **Fairness.** If you're hoping to meet someone, they deserve to see who they're meeting. That goes both ways.

Every profile is also approved by the admin before it goes live, so a clear main photo saves you a round of changes.

## What about sunglasses and hats?

Sunglasses, caps and hats are common in outdoor photos. They're fine in your other photos, such as a run on the beach or a hike. They're not fine for the main photo, because they shade the face.

The same goes for heavy shadow, a scarf pulled over your mouth or a hood pulled low. If part of your face is hidden, it isn't a clear face photo.

2 quick fixes:

- Take your main photo in the shade or on an overcast day, so you don't need sunglasses.
- Push your cap back, or take it off for one shot.

Keep the favourite sunny shot for slot 2 or 3. Then you get both.

## It has to be you

The main photo needs to be a photo of the member, with the member as the main person in it. A friend, a pet, a car or a group of 6 doesn't count, even if you're somewhere in the back. Photos of other people also need their consent, and group shots make it hard for a reader to know which one is you.

Be careful with heavy filters and editing, too. A filter that changes your face makes you harder to recognise, which defeats the point. Light touches like cropping or brightness are fine.

If you're not comfortable with how you look in photos, that's very common. Ask a friend you trust to take a few if that's easier, since it often takes the pressure off. Otherwise, take a bunch of shots in good light, delete the ones you dislike and pick the one where you look most like you on a good day. It doesn't have to be perfect. It has to be you.

## Where can you take one?

Easy settings include:

- Near a window at home, facing the light
- In the shade outside your gym
- A park bench on an overcast morning
- A mirror selfie with the phone held away from your face

That last one needs care. If the phone covers your face, use that shot for a different slot. ## How does a clear photo help everyone?

This part isn't only about you.

When every main photo clearly shows a real face, the whole thing gets easier to trust. The reader can see who they're looking at. A clear photo isn't proof of anything on its own, though, so if you want to know what else to look for, [how to spot a fake profile](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-profile/) lists them.

It also means that if your link gets passed along, the person reading it can connect it to someone they might already know at the gym. That's the point of the whole setup.
