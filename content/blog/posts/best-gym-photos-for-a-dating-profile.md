The best gym photos for a dating profile are one clear photo of your face, plus a training shot or a post-session picture that shows how you spend your time. Skip anything with bystanders, screenshots, memes or text overlaid on the image. On SetsOfLove you can add 1 to 4 photos, so each one needs to earn its slot.

## How many photos do you need?

You can use 1 to 4. Here's a simple decision rule: add a photo only if it tells the reader something the others don't. 4 photos of you in the same pose with the same shirt is really one photo.

If you only have one good image, use one. A single clear photo beats 4 mediocre ones. You can edit later, though any edit sends a live profile back for approval, so it's worth adding the strong ones together.

## A set that works

Think of the photos as jobs rather than poses. Here's one reliable layout for 4:

| Slot | What it shows | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (main) | Your face, clear, good light | Lets people recognise you |
| 2 | A training shot | Shows what you do |
| 3 | A post-session or kit photo | Shows you off the clock |
| 4 | Somewhere outside the gym | Shows life beyond the rack |

You can run with 3 or 2 slots. If you're keeping it to 2, take slots 1 and 2. The main photo shows your face clearly, and that's the one rule that matters most. See [why your main photo should clearly show your face](/blog/main-photo-show-your-face/) for the reasons.

## Which gym photos work well?

Gym and workout photos are encouraged here, so lean in.

- **A training shot.** Mid-warm-up, setting up a lift or finishing a run on a treadmill. A friend can take it, as long as nobody else is in the frame.
- **A post-session mirror selfie.** Relaxed, a bit flushed, kit on. It's welcome, and [how to take a good mirror selfie for your profile](/blog/mirror-selfie-tips-for-your-profile/) covers the details.
- **Gym kit in daylight.** In the car park, at the park or before a class.
- **A moment from your sport.** A run, a swim, a climb, a hike.

Candid beats posed. A photo where you're mid-laugh will usually tell the reader more than a carefully held stance.

## Which photos should you skip?

Some images get a profile sent back, and others just don't help. Leave these out:

- **Photos with bystanders.** Photos of other people without their consent aren't allowed. Cropping isn't always enough if someone is still recognisable.
- **Group shots as your main photo.** Readers shouldn't have to work out which person is you.
- **Screenshots.** They're not allowed, and they look low effort.
- **Memes and images with text or logos.** Not allowed either, and they hide you.
- **Anything with nudity or anything sexual.** Not allowed, full stop.
- **Old photos.** If you looked different 5 years ago, show how you look now.
- **Blurry or dark photos.** If you can't tell it's you, nobody else can.

[Photo rules: what's welcome and what isn't](/blog/setsoflove-photo-rules/) lists them all in one place.

## Worked example: picking 3 from 10

Say you have 10 candidates on your phone. Run this quick pass.

1. Delete every photo with a bystander, a screenshot or text over the image. Say that cuts 4.
2. From the remaining 6, pick the sharpest one where your face is easy to see. That's your main photo.
3. Pick the clearest training photo. Make sure it isn't a repeat of the main one.
4. Pick one more that adds something new, like an outdoor shot.

You're left with 3 that each have a job. Done in about 5 minutes.

## What about lighting and quality?

Photo quality is mostly about light and focus, and you can fix both for free.

Good light does more than a good camera. Face a window, avoid harsh ceiling lights directly overhead, and don't shoot into a bright background. [Lighting tips for gym photos](/blog/gym-photo-lighting-tips/) has a fix for each common problem.

Check each photo at small size too. Most people will see a profile on their phone, so a photo that looks fine on a laptop can become a grey blob. If you can still read your expression at thumbnail size, it's good.

## A final check

Ask yourself 3 things about each photo. Is it recent? Is it clearly you? Would you be comfortable if a friend of a friend saw it? If you can answer yes to all three, it can go on the profile. If one is a maybe, hold it back and try another. A tight set of 2 or 3 strong photos reads better than 4 where one is a compromise.
