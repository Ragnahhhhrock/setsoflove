If you have a gym crush, the calmest plan is to start with a nod, move to a short hello, spend a few weeks being a friendly regular and only then consider a simple, no-pressure invitation. Respect their space throughout, and stop at the first sign they're not interested. Their comfort at the gym comes before your plan.

## What does a low-pressure plan look like?

Having a crush is normal, and it doesn't make you creepy. What matters is what you do with it. A good plan has 5 steps, each one small, each one easy for the other person to ignore or decline without any fuss.

1. A nod.
2. A short hello.
3. A few weeks of friendly regularity.
4. A simple invitation, or a mutual-friend route.
5. A clear stopping point.

You don't need to complete every step. Many crushes fade at step 2, and that's fine. Some turn into friendships. A few turn into dates. All of those are good outcomes.

## Step 1: how do you start with a nod?

Make eye contact when it happens naturally, smile slightly and carry on with your session. That's it.

Keep it casual. Don't stare, don't position yourself where you can watch them and don't time your sessions to theirs. Train when you normally train. If you see each other at similar times, great. If you don't, don't change your schedule to make it happen.

If they nod back, you've got a small friendly signal. If they look away, treat that as information and leave it there.

## Step 2: how do you say a short hello?

After a week or two of nods, a short hello is a natural next move. Wait until they're between sets and not wearing headphones. Then keep it to one sentence about something you're both doing.

- "Morning."
- "Is that bench free after you?"
- "Busy one today, isn't it?"

Leave the other person an easy exit. Say it, smile and carry on. Don't linger for a reply, and don't expect more than a "Yeah" or a nod. For more on timing and cues, see [how to talk to someone at the gym without being awkward](/blog/how-to-talk-to-someone-at-the-gym/).

Avoid comments about their body, their lifts or their outfit. Even kind ones can feel like being sized up. And never touch them to get their attention.

## Step 3: what does a few weeks of friendly regularity involve?

This is the longest step, and the most useful. Over 3 to 6 weeks, aim to become a pleasant, predictable part of their gym day.

- Say a quick hello when you cross paths.
- Chat for 30 seconds at most, and only when they seem happy to.
- Use their name once you know it.
- Be easy to share a space with: wipe down, re-rack, wait your turn.
- Treat everyone else the same way. Warmth toward only one person feels strange.

Watch for two-way signs. Do they start conversations? Do they ask you something back? Do they look pleased to see you? If it's always you making the effort, take that as your answer. [How to tell if someone wants to talk at the gym](/blog/how-to-tell-if-someone-wants-to-chat-at-the-gym/) covers the cues in more detail.

Also notice whether you simply enjoy this person's company. If you do, you're building a friendship, which is worth having whatever else happens.

## Step 4: how do you invite them, or use a mutual friend?

If the conversations are warm and two-way after several weeks, you can try one simple invitation. Pick a moment when they're finished and free, in a place where they can easily leave. Keep it plain and optional:

- "I've enjoyed chatting at the gym. Would you like to grab a coffee sometime? No worries if not."
- "A few of us are heading for a walk after Saturday's class. Want to join?"

Ask once. Accept any answer, including a vague one like "Maybe sometime" or "I'm busy", as a no.

If you'd rather avoid a face-to-face question in a shared space, there are softer paths:

- **A mutual friend.** If you both know someone, they can mention you and see whether the other person is open to it. Both of you can answer in private. If you're the friend in the middle, check that both people want an introduction before saying anything.
- **A group plan.** Invite several regulars to something casual, so nobody is singled out.
- **A profile link.** SetsOfLove lets single gym-goers aged 18 or over create a profile at their own link. Members choose who they'd like to meet and what they're after, and they decide where to share the link. There are no messages, so nothing lands in someone's inbox unasked. It only works if both people have chosen to be there.

## Step 5: when should you stop?

Stopping is part of the plan, not a failure of it. Stop if:

- They say no, or give any answer that sounds like no.
- They give short, flat replies for more than a couple of weeks.
- They look uncomfortable, move away or put headphones on when you approach.
- They tell you, or the staff tell you, that they'd like space.
- You notice yourself adjusting your whole schedule around theirs.

When you stop, you stop completely. Keep your own routine, offer a plain nod if you pass and let the crush run its course. Don't ask others about them, don't try again later and don't look for them elsewhere. If it stings, that's normal. ## What if the crush is making training harder?

A crush can make your head noisy. If you're finding it hard to focus on your session, scale back your attention to it. Remind yourself that you came to train. Move to a different part of the gym if you need to, or switch to a time that suits you better.

If thoughts about someone start to feel consuming or distressing, talk to a friend, or contact your GP or [Beyond Blue](https://www.beyondblue.org.au/). That's a normal thing to do, and it's nothing to be embarrassed about.

The simple version is this: be kind, be patient, ask at most once and let them decide. Plans like that tend to leave everyone, including you, feeling good.
