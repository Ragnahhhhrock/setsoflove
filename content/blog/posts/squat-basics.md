title: Squat basics: setup, depth and common mistakes
description: Squat form for beginners: stance, bracing, depth and the common mistakes to avoid. Learn with light loads and get a qualified coach to check you.
---
Good squat form comes down to a stance that feels natural, a braced trunk and a depth you can control. Start with bodyweight or a light load, keep your whole foot on the floor and let your knees track in line with your toes. A qualified coach is the best way to learn, because squats look different on every body.

## How do you set up for good squat form?

Whether you squat with a bar or without one, the setup is the same idea.

1. **Pick a stance.** Feet roughly shoulder width apart, toes turned out a little, is a common starting point. Your own stance may be a bit wider or narrower.
2. **Set the rack height.** If you're using a bar, it should sit around mid-chest height so you can unrack it without tiptoeing or dipping.
3. **Get under the bar.** Place it across your upper back, on the muscle shelf, not on the bone of your neck. Hold it with both hands and keep your elbows in a comfortable position.
4. **Stand up, step back and settle.** Take as few steps as you can. Then reset your feet.
5. **Pick a spot to look at.** Straight ahead or slightly down is fine. Keep your head in line with your spine.

If the bar hurts your shoulders or wrists, ask a coach about hand position or about a different bar placement. Pad the bar only if your coach suggests it.

## What does bracing mean?

Bracing is how you keep your trunk firm so your spine stays steady under load. It's the part beginners most often skip, and it matters for the squat and the other big lifts.

- **Breathe in low.** Fill your belly and sides, not just your chest.
- **Tighten around your middle.** Imagine someone is about to tap your stomach.
- **Hold that air and tension while you go down and come back up.** Breathe out at or near the top, then reset before the next rep.
- **Keep your ribs stacked over your hips.** Don't flare your ribs or let your lower back arch hard.

It takes practice. Try it with no weight first, then add the bar. A coach can show you what it should feel like.

## How low should you squat?

Depth is a personal thing, and it depends on your hips, ankles, the way your body is built and what you're training for. There's no single right answer.

- **Start with a depth you can control.** If you can't slow down on the way down, you've gone too deep or too heavy.
- **Keep your back in a steady position.** If your lower back rounds or tucks under at the bottom, that's your cue to stop going lower for now.
- **Build depth over time.** Mobility and strength both improve with practice. Many people squat deeper after a few months.
- **Try a box or bench to give yourself a target.** Sitting back to a box is a common learning tool.

A squat that's a bit shallower but steady is better than a deeper one that's sloppy.

## What are the common squat mistakes?

- **Heels lifting off the floor.** This can be a sign of tight ankles or of leaning forward. Ask a coach to look at it.
- **Knees caving inward.** Think about pushing your knees out in line with your toes as you stand up.
- **Hips shooting up first.** If your hips rise faster than your chest, the weight is probably too heavy.
- **Rounding at the bottom.** Reduce the depth or the load and rebuild.
- **Holding your breath without bracing, or not bracing at all.** Practise the breathing pattern above.
- **Looking up at the ceiling.** It can crank your neck. Pick a neutral gaze.
- **Adding weight too quickly.** This is the biggest one for everyone, and the easiest to fix.

Notice that most of these are fixed by lowering the load. That's not failing. It's how you learn.

## How should you stay safe under the bar?

Squats can go wrong in a few ways, so set up your safety before you start.

- **Use safety arms or pins in a squat rack.** Set them just below your lowest squat, so you can lower the bar onto them if you get stuck.
- **Learn how to step out safely.** A coach can teach you what to do if you can't stand up.
- **Don't squat to your limit alone.** Keep some reps in hand.
- **Wear flat, stable shoes if you can.** Soft, cushioned soles can make your balance wobbly under load.
- **Clear the floor around you.** Plates, bags and bottles are trip hazards.

## How do you progress safely?

Start with bodyweight squats or a goblet squat holding a dumbbell at your chest. This is a common beginner option, because it gives you a counterbalance and builds a feel for the position. Once that feels steady, ask a coach about moving to a bar.

Then add load slowly. Add it only when your last session felt controlled, and drop back if form slips. Write down what you did, because it helps you see what's working. [How to track your lifts](/blog/how-to-track-your-lifts/) covers what's worth recording.

Rest matters too. Your legs adapt between sessions, not during them. Some soreness is normal. Sharp pain isn't.

## When should you see a professional?

Learn the squat with a qualified coach if you can. They can watch from the side and front, spot patterns you can't feel and adjust your stance and depth to your body. Many gyms offer a coaching session or an induction, and it's worth the money.

If you feel sharp pain, pain that doesn't ease or numbness or tingling, stop and see a doctor or physio. Don't wait for it to go away on its own.
