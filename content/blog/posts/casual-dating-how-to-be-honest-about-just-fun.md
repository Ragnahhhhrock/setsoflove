title: Casual dating: how to be honest about 'just fun'
description: Casual dating honesty means saying clearly that you want something light, respecting the other person's answer and checking in as things change.
---
Casual dating honesty comes down to 3 things: say clearly that you want something light, ask what the other person wants, and respect the answer. "Just fun" is a fair thing to want. It only works when everyone knows the terms and can change their mind at any point.

## What "just fun" means

"Just fun" is shorthand, and shorthand gets misread. To one person it means occasional dates with no plan. To another it means good company, a coffee or a walk, with no commitment. To a third it means something they haven't really thought about.

That's why it helps to be specific. Before you tell anyone, work out what it means for you:

- How often would you like to see someone?
- Are you fine with them seeing other people?
- What would make you want to stop?
- Would you still want to be friendly afterwards?

You don't need perfect answers. You need enough to explain yourself in a sentence or two.

## Say it early and kindly

Clear beats clever. If you want something light, say so before anyone's feelings are involved. A first or second date is a good time. If your profile already says "just fun", that's a start, but it isn't the same as saying it to a person.

Some wording that works:

- "I'm enjoying this. I should say I'm not looking for anything serious right now."
- "I'd like to keep things relaxed. Is that what you're after too?"
- "I like your company and I'd like to keep seeing you. I'm not ready for a relationship."

Notice that each sentence describes you and leaves space for them. None of them is a trap or a test.

Avoid hinting and hoping they'll pick it up. Avoid saying "I'm easy, whatever you want" if that isn't true. And avoid framing "casual" as a way to keep options open while they believe it's exclusive.

## Ask, then listen

Once you've said your piece, ask what they'd like. Then take the answer at face value.

Some responses you might hear:

1. **"That suits me too."** Good. Check in occasionally anyway.
2. **"I'd like something more serious."** Take them at their word. If you don't want that, step back kindly. Offering "casual for now and we'll see" when you know you won't change your mind isn't fair.
3. **"I'm not sure."** Give them room and don't push for a quick answer. A pause is fine.
4. **"I'd rather just be friends."** That's a clear answer, and you can respect it.

None of these is wrong. A difference in what you want is no one's fault. It's a reason to part kindly, or to be friends. If it's useful, [the guide to knowing what you're after](/blog/what-are-you-looking-for/) helps you put your own answer into words first.

## Consent covers more than the obvious

Consent isn't a one-off. In casual dating it means each person is freely choosing, every time, with the full picture.

In practice:

- **Honest information.** Don't hide things that would change their choice, like seeing other people if they'd expect exclusivity.
- **Free choice.** A yes given under pressure, or out of fear of upsetting you, isn't a real yes. If someone seems unsure, ask, and be fine with a no.
- **A no is final.** You don't need a reason, and you don't ask them to justify it.
- **Anyone can change their mind.** Including you, including midway through a plan, and with no explanation owed.
- **Respect privacy.** Don't share their photos or details without asking.

If you're not sure how to say your limits, [setting boundaries early when you start dating](/blog/setting-boundaries-early-when-you-start-dating/) has wording you can borrow.

## When someone changes their mind

Feelings shift. You might start the way you meant to and then find you want more. Or the other person might.

If that happens to you:

- **Say it soon.** A short, kind message beats weeks of drifting.
- **Say it without blame.** "My feelings have changed and I'd like to talk about it" is enough.
- **Accept that they might not feel the same.** They may want to carry on as before, or to stop. Both are fair.

If it happens to them:

- **Thank them for telling you.**
- **Don't argue them out of it.**
- **Take what you need.** You can say, "I'd like a few days to think."

If it stings, [how to handle rejection gracefully](/blog/how-to-handle-rejection-gracefully/) has a plan for the first few days.

## Ending it well

Casual doesn't mean careless. When you'd like to stop, say so plainly and warmly. Avoid vanishing.

A message might read: "I've enjoyed spending time with you. I don't think this is going where I'd like, so I'm going to stop here. Thank you, and I wish you well."

It's short and kind, and it doesn't blame. If you share a gym, keep it friendly. A nod and a hello after the fact makes everyone's life easier.
