title: Dating again after a long relationship
description: Dating after a long relationship takes time. Learn how to check you're ready, update your profile, talk about the past briefly and build new routines.
---
Dating after a long relationship goes better when you move at your own pace and deal with the practical bits first: a fresh profile, recent photos and a short, calm answer about your past. There's no deadline. Being ready is less about how much time has passed and more about whether you can talk about the old relationship without it taking over.

## Are you ready? A straight check

There's no test for this, but a few honest questions help.

- Can I talk about my ex in a calm, short way?
- Am I curious about meeting someone, or am I mainly trying to avoid being alone?
- Could I enjoy a coffee with someone without needing it to go anywhere?
- Do I have people and routines that keep me steady outside of dating?

If you answered yes to most, go ahead. If you answered no to a few, that's information, not a failure. You can start small, with friendships and social outings, and let the rest follow.

If you're feeling flat or overwhelmed for longer than you'd expect, talk to your doctor or a counsellor. [Beyond Blue](https://www.beyondblue.org.au/) is a good place to start for support.

## Take your time

Dating again can feel like a skill you've forgotten. It's normal to be rusty. Early dates may feel awkward, and that's fine.

Some ways to keep the pace gentle:

- Set a loose pace, like one coffee a week, rather than a full diary.
- Spend time with friends alongside dating, not instead of it.
- Give yourself permission to say "not yet" to an invitation.
- Notice how you feel afterwards. Tired is information. Curious is too.

You're allowed to stop for a month and come back later. Nothing is lost.

## Update your profile and photos

If your last profile or last set of photos is from years ago, start fresh. People will want to meet who you are now.

For photos:

1. Take 2 to 4 new ones. A clear face photo for the main image, plus a gym or training shot and one of something you enjoy.
2. Leave out anything that includes your ex or other people. On SetsOfLove, photos of others without their consent aren't allowed.
3. Skip anything old enough that you'd look different today.

For the written parts, describe your life as it is now. What do you do? How do you train? What's on your weekends? The "about you" field is the place for one or two details that sound like you. If you're stuck for words, the tappable quick picks give you a starting point, and you can edit all of it.

Remember that any edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again. Get your changes ready in one go, as [how often to update your profile photos](/blog/how-often-to-update-profile-photos/) explains.

## Talk about the past briefly

You'll be asked about your last relationship. Have a short answer ready. It doesn't need to be dramatic or secretive.

A few examples:

- "We were together 11 years. It ended last year, and we're on decent terms."
- "It ran its course. I've spent the time since getting my own routine back."
- "It was a long one, and I'm glad about a lot of it. I'm ready to meet new people."

Then ask something about them. Keep your side to two or three sentences on a first date. Save the longer story for later, when you know and trust each other.

Avoid criticising your ex. Even if it's justified, it tends to make the listener wonder how you'll talk about them one day. Avoid comparing the person in front of you with your ex too.

## Build new routines

Long relationships shape your week. You may have trained at a certain time because of someone else's schedule, or skipped sessions to fit shared plans. Now is a chance to reset.

Look at your week and ask:

- Which sessions do I want to keep, whatever else happens?
- Is there a class or time slot I always avoided that I'd like to try?
- What can I do on evenings and weekends that I enjoy, whether or not I'm dating?

A steady training routine helps. It gives you structure and a place to see familiar faces. Just make sure it isn't a way to avoid everything else. [Explaining your routine to a date](/blog/explaining-your-routine-to-a-date/) can help when you start sharing your schedule with someone new.

## Be kind to yourself about the awkward bits

Some dates won't go anywhere. Someone might not reply, or you might feel nothing. This happens to everyone, and it says little about you. If it stings more than you expected, [how to handle rejection gracefully](/blog/how-to-handle-rejection-gracefully/) has some practical steps.

Also consider what you're hoping for. After a long relationship, some people want a new partner quickly. Others want friendship, company and room to breathe. [Knowing what you're after](/blog/what-are-you-looking-for/) makes the whole process clearer.

## A simple plan for the first month

1. Update your photos and your profile in one sitting.
2. Decide what you're after and write it down.
3. Choose one low-key plan a week, such as a coffee and a walk.
4. Keep your training times.
5. At the end of the month, check in with yourself.

Keep it small. Pace matters more than speed, and you can always go faster later.
