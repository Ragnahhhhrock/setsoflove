---
title: January gym crowds: etiquette and patience
description: January gym crowds are temporary. A practical guide to etiquette, sharing equipment, quieter hours and a workable plan for newcomers and regulars.
---
January gym crowds are real, and they settle down. For the first few weeks of the year, expect queues for popular equipment, busier classes and a car park that fills early. Share equipment, ask politely, try a quieter hour and be welcoming to newcomers. Everyone is there for the same reason.

## Why is the gym so busy in January?

Many people use the new year as a fresh start, and in Australia it also lines up with the end of the holiday break and the start of the school and work year. The result is more people on the floor, especially in the early morning, after work and on weekday evenings.

The busiest stretch usually eases over several weeks. In the meantime, a little patience and a few adjustments go a long way. Regulars were new once, and newcomers will be regulars soon.

## What should newcomers know?

If you're new, welcome. You belong here. A few things make the first weeks smoother:

- **Do a gym induction.** Ask at the front desk. It's the quickest way to learn where things are and how the machines work.
- **Have a plan.** A short written list saves you hovering around the floor. [How to start strength training](/blog/how-to-start-strength-training/) has a simple first session to borrow.
- **Ask when you're unsure.** "Are you using this?" and "How long are you on it for?" are normal questions.
- **Be flexible.** If a machine is taken, do another movement first and come back.
- **Wipe down equipment** after you use it. Most gyms provide towels or spray.

If the crowds feel overwhelming, you aren't alone. [Gym anxiety: how to walk in with more confidence](/blog/gym-anxiety-how-to-walk-in-with-confidence/) has practical ideas for settling in.

## What should regulars remember?

Your routine may be disrupted for a few weeks. That's annoying, and it's also temporary. Some habits keep things friendly:

- **Be kind to people who look unsure.** A short "it's all yours" or a quick explanation of how a machine adjusts can make someone's day.
- **Don't comment on how people train.** Offer advice only when asked. Everyone is learning.
- **Don't stare or film.** Keep your attention on your own workout.
- **Return weights to the rack.** Leaving a loaded bar for someone else to strip is a classic January frustration.
- **Keep your gear tidy.** Bags and water bottles belong against the wall, not on benches or in walkways.

A dry note for the regulars: the equipment you've had to yourself since October is now a shared resource. That's the whole deal.

## How do you share equipment fairly?

Busy days call for a simple etiquette:

1. **Work in** if someone is resting between sets. Ask first: "Mind if I work in?" Then adjust the settings and put them back afterwards.
2. **Rest away from the equipment.** Sit on a nearby bench or stand to the side so others can see it's free.
3. **Limit phone time** on a bench or machine. Scrolling between sets blocks a busy spot.
4. **Don't claim several stations.** One piece of equipment at a time is fair.
5. **Be patient with a queue.** If someone's waiting, keep your rest periods short.
6. **Wait for people mid-set.** Don't interrupt, and keep clear of their line of movement.

If you want to talk to someone while you're waiting, keep it light and short, and notice whether they seem keen. Chatting is welcome in general. Interrupting someone's set isn't.

## When are the quieter hours?

Every gym has its own rhythm, but quieter times are often mid-morning, early afternoon and later in the evening. Weekends can vary a lot, with mornings often busier than late afternoons. Ask the staff what they've noticed or watch the floor for a couple of visits.

If you can't change your schedule, here are some adjustments:

- Arrive slightly earlier than the rush, so you finish before the peak.
- Choose a less popular piece of equipment for the same job, such as dumbbells instead of a crowded rack.
- Shorten your session on the busiest days and keep the minimum.
- Try a class, which gives you a set time and space.

A gym's most crowded spots tend to be the squat racks, benches and cardio machines, so the free areas, machines and stretching zones may offer breathing room.

## How do you build a plan that works in a crowd?

Flexibility is the secret. Write your session as a list of movements, not a fixed order, so you can swap when a station is taken. Have a backup option for each main movement. If the bench is busy, do a different push first.

Keep your sessions shorter and more focused while it's busy. A 30-minute visit that goes to plan beats an hour of waiting around. [Consistency over motivation](/blog/consistency-over-motivation/) explains how to keep showing up when your usual routine is disrupted.

## How long will January gym crowds last?

Not forever. Attendance usually tapers off over the weeks that follow, though some newcomers stay and become part of the community. The gym will look more like itself again soon enough.

If you see someone who's trying to build a habit, a smile or a short "good on you for coming in" is a kind way to help. And if you're one of the new faces, a quick thanks to the regulars who made space goes a long way.

Be patient, be tidy and be kind. January only lasts 31 days.
