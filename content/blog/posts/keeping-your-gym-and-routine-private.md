title: Keeping your gym and routine private when you date

---
For dating privacy, keep your exact routine and your gym to yourself until you've met someone a few times and feel comfortable. Your schedule shows where you will be and when you'll be alone, so share it slowly. A simple rule is to share by trust, not by time spent chatting.

## Why your routine is worth protecting

When you train at the same place at the same time, you have a pattern. Most people who learn it will do nothing with it. But a pattern is information, and information is easier to give than to take back. A new acquaintance who knows your gym, your 6:00 am sessions and your usual route home can turn up in your space without being invited.

This isn't a reason to be suspicious of everyone. It's a reason to treat your routine like your home address: something you share deliberately, when you want to.

Your gym is also your own space. Many people value it as a place where they aren't being looked at or approached. Protecting it keeps it that way.

## What to hold back early

In the first few conversations, these are worth keeping general:

- **Which gym you go to.** "I train near the city" is enough.
- **Your exact times.** "Early mornings" is plenty. "5:30 am on Monday, Wednesday and Friday" is a schedule.
- **Your route.** Where you park, how you travel and which door you use.
- **Your home suburb and street.** Share the broad area when it helps, not the address.
- **Your workplace.** A job type is fine. Your employer and building aren't needed yet.
- **Your plans.** Skip real-time posts about where you are right now, and avoid saying when you'll be home alone.

You can still be warm and open. Talk about what you enjoy, what you're working on and what your week feels like, without handing over coordinates.

## A decision rule for when to share more

Instead of guessing, use a few checkpoints. Share the next layer of detail when all of these are true:

1. You've met in person at least a couple of times.
2. They've respected every limit you've set, including small ones.
3. You feel at ease rather than curious about why they keep asking.
4. A friend knows who they are.

If one of these isn't true, wait. Waiting costs very little, and anyone who wants to get to know you will understand. You can say it in one line: "I keep my gym and schedule private until I know someone better."

## How to say no without making it awkward

You don't need to invent an excuse. A calm, short answer is enough:

- "I'd rather not say which gym. I like keeping it separate."
- "My times change a lot. I'll let you know when I know."
- "I'm keeping that private for now."

Then change the subject to something else. If someone asks again and again after you've answered, that's information too. Our post on [setting boundaries early when you start dating](/blog/setting-boundaries-early-when-you-start-dating/) has more phrases and what to do if a limit is ignored.

## Arrange first meetings so your routine stays private

How you meet matters as much as what you say.

- **Choose a neutral public place** that isn't your gym or near your home, like a cafe in the middle of town.
- **Pick a time that doesn't fall on your usual session days.** A first meeting that lands right after your regular training makes your pattern easy to see.
- **Travel separately** and head home separately, ideally not straight to your front door if you're unsure.
- **Tell a friend** where you're going. Our [first date safety checklist](/blog/first-date-safety-checklist/) covers this in more detail.
- **Hold off on meeting at the gym** until you know and trust the person. If it happens, a quick conversation in the foyer is enough.

## Privacy on your profile and social accounts

What you post publicly can give away the same details you're holding back in conversation. Check these:

- Photos that show the gym's name, a logo or a distinctive building.
- Photos with a location tag or a recognisable street behind you.
- Posts that show a regular time or place, like a weekly class.
- Public accounts that show check-ins or live locations.
- Photos with other people in them. They haven't agreed to be part of your profile.

On SetsOfLove, phone numbers and social handles aren't allowed in public text fields, and photos with text or logos aren't accepted. Each profile has a first name, age, suburb, city and country, and you choose what to write in the other fields. Keep the "how you train" field general, such as "morning weights", and leave out your gym and session times. Search engines are told not to list profiles, and members decide where to share their link. ## If someone already knows too much

If someone seems to know more about your routine than you've told them, or turns up where you train without being asked, take it seriously.

- Note what you've noticed and when.
- Tell the gym staff and, if you want, ask them to keep an eye out.
- Change your times for a while if it makes you feel better.
- Stop sharing new details and stop replying if you're uncomfortable.

If you're concerned about online behaviour, [eSafety](https://www.esafety.gov.au/) has information and a way to report. If you ever feel in danger, call 000. Our post on [handling unwanted attention at the gym](/blog/handling-unwanted-attention-at-the-gym/) covers what to do next.

Your routine is yours. Share it when you want to, and not before.
