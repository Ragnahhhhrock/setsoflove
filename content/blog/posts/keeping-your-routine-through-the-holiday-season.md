To keep your gym routine over Christmas and the summer holidays, set a minimum plan you can always manage and treat everything above it as a bonus. Check your own gym's holiday hours now, expect a crowded calendar and pick dates that fit the season. A short session on a busy day still counts.

## What changes at the end of the year in Australia

December in Australia is summer, school holidays and end-of-year events all at once. Your usual week gets pushed around.

Common changes include:

- **Gym hours.** Some gyms change their hours around public holidays and the break. Check your own gym's holiday hours on its website or at the front desk, and don't assume they look like your normal week.
- **Classes.** Timetables can change or pause. Ask the gym what is running.
- **Work.** Team lunches, deadlines before the break and leave can all shift your schedule.
- **Family and friends.** Gatherings, travel and visitors take up evenings and weekends.
- **Heat.** Training outdoors in the middle of the day is rarely a good idea. The [training in an Australian summer](/blog/training-in-an-australian-summer/) post has sensible advice.

None of this is a problem, as long as you plan for it. The trouble starts when you pretend it's a normal week.

## Set a minimum routine

A minimum routine is the least you will do in a week, however busy it gets. It should be small enough that you can do it in your worst week.

Here is a way to build one.

1. Look at your normal week. If you train 4 or 5 times, note how many sessions you'd really miss.
2. Cut that to a number you're confident about. For many people, that's 2 sessions.
3. Decide the length. A 30-minute session is plenty.
4. Choose a fallback for each session, such as a walk, a swim or a home bodyweight circuit.
5. Write the sessions in your calendar like appointments.

If you hit your minimum, count it as a win. Anything extra is a bonus. This takes away the all-or-nothing thinking that leads people to drop everything from mid-December to February.

For a longer look at building habits that stick, the [balancing training and a relationship](/blog/balancing-training-and-a-relationship/) post covers the calendar conversation.

## Plan around the gaps

Once you know the minimum, look ahead.

- **Mark the fixed events.** Work parties, family days and travel go in first.
- **Slot sessions around them.** Choose the quiet mornings and the spare afternoons.
- **Pick a plan B for each week.** If the gym is closed, where will you train instead?
- **Train early or late.** Summer mornings are cooler and often calmer.
- **Pack a bag the night before.** It is easy to skip a session when you're hunting for clean socks.

If your gym is shut for a few days, a park, a beach or a local pool can fill the gap. Walk, jog, swim or do a bodyweight circuit. You don't need to replace the full session. You just need to keep moving.

## Eat, drink and don't moralise

This is a season of long lunches and rich food. There's no need to treat that as a problem. Enjoy it. Training doesn't have to cancel out a meal, and food isn't a reward or a punishment.

If you want a rule, the only one worth having is to look after yourself. Drink water in the heat. Sleep when you can. Don't use a session to make up for a party.

## Dating over the summer break

The holidays can be a good time to meet someone. People are relaxed, days are long and there are plenty of outdoor options. They can also be a hard time, because everyone's diary is full.

A few thoughts that help.

- **Be honest about availability.** "I'm away from 23 Dec to 4 Jan, but free the week before" is a clear sentence.
- **Choose simple plans.** Coffee, a swim or a sunset walk fits into a packed week. Look at the [summer dates by the water in Perth](/blog/summer-dates-by-the-water-in-perth/) post for ideas.
- **Don't expect a quick pace.** People are away, tired and busy. A slow reply isn't always disinterest.
- **Mind the heat and the sun.** Bring water and sunscreen, and choose shade or early starts.
- **Be careful with big events.** A work party or a family gathering is not a place to bring someone you've only met twice. Wait until you both want it.

If you're seeing someone already, plan the shared bits early. Which events will you attend together? Which will you each do alone? Which days belong to your own training? A short chat saves a lot of tension.

## Keeping your sleep steady

Sleep is the first thing to go at this time of year. Late nights, travel and heat all take a toll.

You don't have to be strict. Choose 2 or 3 nights a week where you aim for your normal bedtime, and let the rest flex. Train early on those mornings if you can. It is easier to get up for a session when you went to bed on time.

If you're sleeping badly for weeks, or feel unwell, see a doctor. For general health information, https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/ is a good place to start.

## A simple holiday plan

Try this layout for the break.

| Item | Your plan |
|---|---|
| Minimum sessions per week | 2, each 30 minutes |
| Fallback for each | Walk, swim or bodyweight circuit |
| Gym hours | Checked directly with your gym |
| Dates or social plans | Written in the calendar early |
| Rest | At least 1 full day, and 2 relaxed evenings |

Review it after the first week. Change what isn't working.

When the break ends, you'll go back to a normal routine without the guilt. If you want to ease back in, the [dating when you train at 5 am](/blog/dating-when-you-train-at-5am/) post has tips on protecting early sessions without being rigid.

Enjoy the long days. A routine is there to support your life, not to get in its way.
