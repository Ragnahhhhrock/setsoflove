Your profile link is the short name after setsoflove.com/, like setsoflove.com/sam-t. The best profile link name ideas use your first name plus an initial or a number, written in lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, between 3 and 30 characters. Leave out surnames, phone numbers and anything you wouldn't say to a stranger.

## What are the rules for a profile link?

Every approved profile lives at its own link, so the name needs to be tidy and easy to type. The rules are short:

- Lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens only.
- Between 3 and 30 characters.
- No full surnames, no phone numbers and nothing offensive.
- Reserved words such as admin, login, signup and api are blocked, along with similar ones.

No spaces, no underscores, no capitals, no full stops. If you type "Sam T." you'll need to turn it into sam-t. Hyphens are your friend: they keep two words readable without any fuss.

## Which profile link name ideas work well?

Most good links follow one of a handful of patterns. Pick the one that sounds most like how a friend would say your name out loud.

| Pattern | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| First name and last initial | sam-t | Short, clean and easy to read out |
| First name and a number | tom-42 | Easy to remember if the number means something to you |
| First name and suburb or city | jo-fremantle | Helps people place you without an address |
| First name and a training word | kit-lifts | Says something about you in one hop |
| Nickname and an initial | jo-r | Fits if everyone already calls you by it |

A few notes on the table. If you use a number, choose one that isn't private. A birth year gives away more than you might think, so a lucky number or a favourite lift number is safer. If you use a suburb, check you're happy for people to know it. Suburb and city already appear on a profile, so this adds nothing new, but you may prefer to keep the link itself plain.

## What should you leave out of your link?

Think of the link as something you'll paste into group chats and say out loud. A few things don't belong in it:

- **Your full surname.** A first name and an initial is plenty.
- **Phone numbers or handles.** Contact details aren't allowed in public text, and a link isn't the place to sneak one in.
- **Your workplace or gym name.** It points people to where you are at a set time of day.
- **Your exact routine.** A link like sam-5am-squats tells strangers when to find you.
- **Anything you'd cringe at in 6 months.** Inside jokes age quickly.

The privacy side is covered in more detail in [what not to put on a dating profile](/blog/what-not-to-put-on-your-profile/). The short version: your link should introduce you, not locate you.

## How do you pick one in 2 minutes?

Use a simple decision rule. Start with your first name. If it's 3 characters or more, add a hyphen and one initial. If that doesn't feel right, swap the initial for a number you're happy to share. If your name is very common and you want something more distinctive, add a word from your training life, like a lift or a class you enjoy.

Here's a worked example. Say someone is called Jordan Lee, trains at 5 am and lives in Joondalup. Their options:

1. jordan-l is clean and safe.
2. jordan-lifts is friendly and tells people what they're into.
3. jordan-joondalup repeats a suburb that's already on the profile, so it's fine but not needed.
4. jordan-lee-5am gives away a surname and a schedule, so it's the one to skip.

They'd go with the first or second. Both are readable, neither reveals more than the profile already does, and both pass the rules without a second look.

## What if your first choice isn't accepted?

If your first pick isn't accepted, don't fight it. Change the ending, not the start. Try a different initial, add a number, or swap in a word from your training life. Avoid piling on extra characters just to get through, because a link that runs near 30 characters is awkward to share and easy to mistype.

Remember that the link is the thing people actually see and pass around. Whoever you send it to will read it before they've seen a single photo, so it sets a small first impression. Warm and plain beats clever.

## Before you save it

Read your link out loud as if you were telling a mate over a set of rows. If it sounds natural, you're done. Then run through a last check:

- Does it use only lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens?
- Is it between 3 and 30 characters?
- Is there a surname, phone number or routine detail hiding in it?
- Would you be comfortable seeing it in a group chat?

Once your profile is approved, you decide where the link goes.
