title: How to introduce two friends who both train
description: Want to introduce friends for dating? Ask each one privately first, keep it light, share a profile link only with consent, then step back.
---
If you want to introduce friends who might date, ask each of them privately first, keep the introduction light, and then step back. Nobody should find out they're being set up while they're already standing next to the other person. Your job is to open a door, not to walk anyone through it.

## Ask each person first, separately

Before you say a word to both of them, check with each one alone. Do it in a way that makes "no" as easy as "yes".

Try something like: "I know someone who trains around your hours and is easy to talk to. Would you be open to meeting them, or would you rather I left it?"

A few rules for this step:

- Ask in private, not in a group chat or in front of other people.
- Say what you'd tell them to their face. Don't pass on gossip, old relationship stories or anything the person told you in confidence.
- Don't send photos, phone numbers or social handles without the person saying yes.
- If you get a no, a maybe or silence, treat all 3 as a no for now.

Some people will say they're not looking. Others will be too polite to refuse. Give everyone a clear way out, such as "No pressure at all, I only thought of you."

## Check that they want the same kind of thing

Two people can both say yes and still want different things. One might be after a relationship while the other wants friendship or a training partner. That isn't a problem if everyone knows it, and it's a real problem if they don't.

If either of them has already said what they're after, you can pass that on, but only with their permission. If they haven't, don't guess. Leave it to them to say it when they're ready.

On SetsOfLove, each member chooses who they'd like to meet and what they're after, in any mix of dates, a relationship, marriage, friendship or just fun. That makes the answer easy to find if both people have a profile. It also means you never have to speak for them.

## How do you introduce friends for dating without it feeling like a setup?

Keep the setting ordinary and the group bigger than 2. A coffee after a Saturday session, a walk with 3 or 4 friends, or a class you all attend works well. The aim is that either person can chat, drift off, or leave without anyone noticing.

A light introduction names something real and stops there:

- "This is Sam. We've trained together for years. Sam, this is Jordan, who lifts Tuesdays and Thursdays."
- "You two both train early. I'll let you compare alarm clocks."

Then do the following:

1. Talk to other people too, so the pair aren't the centre of attention.
2. Don't hint, nudge, wink or mention dating in front of anyone.
3. Don't leave them alone with a pointed "I'll let you get to know each other". Let the conversation form naturally.
4. Leave when you planned to leave. Don't hover to see how it goes.

The aim is that, if nothing happens, both people can go home having had a perfectly nice coffee.

## Sharing a profile link instead of pushing

Some friends would rather look first and decide later. A profile link suits that well. On SetsOfLove, each approved profile lives at its own link, like setsoflove.com/sam-t, and there are no messages, comments or likes. The person looks in their own time, and nobody can pressure them through the app.

That makes a link a gentle option, but a few points matter:

- A profile belongs to the person who made it. Ask before you share their link, even with a friend you trust.
- Send the link with a plain line and no expectation: "Here's Jordan's profile in case you're curious. No need to reply."
- Search engines are told not to list profiles, so people generally find them because a friend shared the link. Your friend's privacy depends on you.
- If someone says no thanks, drop it and don't send it again.

## Make no easy, and mean it

Sometimes one person says they're not interested after meeting or after seeing the link. Your reaction decides whether that stays a small moment or becomes a lingering one.

Good responses:

- "No worries. Thanks for giving it a go."
- "That's fine. Same time on Saturday?"

Bad responses:

- Asking them to reconsider.
- Telling the other person exactly what was said.
- Trying again in 2 weeks "in case they've changed their mind".

If someone hasn't responded, assume that's their answer. Both friends should come away still trusting you.

## Step back, and keep the safety basics

Once the introduction is done, let go. Don't ask for updates, don't check whether they've messaged each other, and don't take sides if things don't work out. If it goes well, be pleased and stay out of it. If it doesn't, stay friends with both.

Introductions through friends can feel more comfortable because there's someone who knows both people. That isn't a guarantee of anything, so encourage the usual habits: meet somewhere public, tell a friend the plan, and arrange their own transport. More detail is in [why introductions through mutual friends can feel safer](/blog/introductions-through-mutual-friends/).

One last thing matters most. If either person later tells you the other made them uncomfortable, listen first and don't defend your instincts as a connector. Believe them, and don't push either of them to give the other a second chance. Being a good connector means looking after both friends, including when it doesn't go as hoped.
