title: How to check if someone's photos are real
description: Simple ways to check if dating photos are real: a reverse image search, a look for inconsistencies, a live video call and trusting your instincts.
---
You can check if dating photos are real with three steps: run a reverse image search, compare the photos against what the person tells you, and talk face to face on a video call before you meet. No single check is proof, but together they catch many problems early.

## Why photos are worth checking

A profile is a few photos and a few lines of text. Photos carry most of the trust, so they are what people borrow, edit or invent when they want to mislead someone. That doesn't mean most people are lying. It means a couple of quick checks cost you almost nothing, and they are easy to do before you've invested time or feelings.

Treat the checks as routine, not as an accusation.

## How to check if photos are real with a reverse image search

A reverse image search looks for other places on the internet where the same picture appears. Most phones and browsers have one built in or available through a search engine's image option.

1. Save or screenshot the photo. Crop out any app buttons so only the picture remains.
2. Upload it to a reverse image search tool.
3. Read the results. Look for the same picture on a different profile, under another name, or on a website that has nothing to do with the person.
4. Repeat with a second and third photo. Scammers often reuse a whole set.

Read the results carefully. If the same face shows up under a different name or a different city, that is a strong warning sign. If the search finds nothing, that doesn't prove the photos are real. A new photo, or one that was never posted anywhere else, simply won't show up. Also, a public figure or a model photo can appear in results for harmless reasons, so look at the context before you decide.

## Look for inconsistencies

Compare the photos with each other and with what the person tells you. Ask yourself:

- Does the person look like the same person across all photos, including build, hair, age and features?
- Do the photos span many years, with nothing recent?
- Do the backgrounds fit the life described? Someone who says they train every morning but has no gym or training photos isn't unusual, but it is worth noticing alongside other things.
- Do the details line up? Age, suburb, job and the places they mention should hold steady from one conversation to the next.
- Do the photos look unusually polished, with nothing casual or candid?

Images made or altered by software can have small oddities. Look at hands, teeth, ears, jewellery, text on signs and the edges of hair or clothing. Warped details are a clue, but the tools keep improving, so don't rely on spotting flaws. A clean-looking photo is not proof that it's genuine.

## Use a video call

A live video call is the most reliable check you have. A short call of 5 to 10 minutes, before any in-person meeting, tells you far more than another round of text.

You can suggest it plainly and without pressure: "Before we meet, do you want to do a quick video call this week? Keeps things easy for both of us."

Pay attention to the response. Someone who is genuine will usually be comfortable with a call, even if they're a little shy about it. Repeated excuses are worth noting, particularly a broken camera, a bad connection, or a reason the call can't happen right now. One missed call is normal. A pattern of avoidance, combined with other flags, is a reason to slow down.

On the call, check that the person you see looks like the photos and that their voice and manner fit the conversation so far.

If you're still unsure, ask for a fresh photo taken today, or ask them to show a detail from their day on the call. Keep it light, and offer the same in return. Never pressure anyone for private images.

## Use the people around you

Mutual friends are one of the best checks there is. If you share a friend, a gym or a training group with someone, ask a person you trust whether they know them. A short answer like "Yes, I've trained with them for a while" counts for a lot. Our post on [introductions through mutual friends](/blog/introductions-through-mutual-friends/) covers why this works and where it falls short.

A friend looking over the profile can also notice what you've talked yourself out of seeing.

## What SetsOfLove does and doesn't check

On SetsOfLove, every profile is approved by the admin before it goes live. The main photo has to show the member's face clearly, and photos can't include other people without consent, screenshots or memes. Those rules help keep profiles clear and tidy.

They aren't an identity check. SetsOfLove doesn't verify who a member is, gym membership isn't checked, and details like lifts are self-reported. Use the same habits you'd use anywhere. If a profile looks wrong, use the "Report this profile" link on the profile. You can also read how to [spot a fake profile](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-profile/) for the wider picture.

## Trust your instincts and know when to stop

If something feels off, you don't need a perfect explanation. You're allowed to pause, ask more questions or stop talking to someone.

Stop and take care if a person:

- refuses every chance to talk by video while pushing the connection forward quickly
- asks for money, gift cards or financial details for any reason
- tells you an urgent story and wants you to act fast

If any of those happen, stop sending anything and read about [romance scam warning signs](/blog/romance-scam-warning-signs/). In Australia, you can report scams to [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/) and contact your bank straight away if you've sent money.

When the checks come back fine, you can relax a little. Keep the usual habits for the first meeting: a public place, a friend who knows your plans and your own transport.
