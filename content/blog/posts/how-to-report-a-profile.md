To report a profile on SetsOfLove, open the profile and select the "Report this profile" link. It goes to the SetsOfLove contact address, where your report is read. Include the profile's link and a clear description of what's wrong, and you've given the admin what they need to look into it.

## When to report a profile

A report is worth sending whenever something on a profile doesn't sit right. You don't need to be certain. Common reasons include:

- A photo of you or someone you know that was used without consent.
- A profile that appears to be impersonating someone.
- Nudity or anything sexual in the photos or text.
- Phone numbers, social handles or other contact details in public text.
- Photos of other people as the main image, or images of people who clearly didn't agree to be there.
- Hateful, discriminatory or obscene wording.
- Content that seems to be a scam, or that asks for money.
- A person who seems to be under 18. SetsOfLove is for people aged 18 or over.

If you're not sure whether something breaks the rules, report it anyway and explain your concern. The rules on photos are set out in the [photo rules](/blog/setsoflove-photo-rules/) post, and the reasoning behind bystander photos is in [consent and gym photos](/blog/consent-and-gym-photos/).

## How to report a dating profile

Every public profile has a "Report this profile" link. Selecting it sends your message to the contact address.

1. Open the profile you're concerned about. Copy its link from your browser, so you have the exact address.
2. Select "Report this profile".
3. Write your message, using the checklist below.
4. Send it. Keep a copy of what you wrote and the date you sent it.

If you can't find the link, or you're reading a screenshot of a profile rather than the live page, you can still use the contact address and include as much detail as you have.

## What to include

A clear report is quicker to act on. Aim for the facts, in a few short lines.

- **The profile link**, for example setsoflove.com followed by the link name.
- **What the problem is**, in one sentence. For example: "The third photo includes another person's face."
- **Where on the profile**, such as which photo or which text field.
- **Why it matters**, if it isn't obvious. For example: "I'm the person in the photo and I didn't agree to it."
- **Anything you've already done**, such as asking the person to remove it.
- **How to reach you**, if you're happy to be contacted for more detail.

Leave out guesses about a person's motives, insults or long histories. A calm, specific report is easier to assess than a long one. If you're reporting something you saw in a conversation elsewhere, such as a message asking for money, keep screenshots of that too.

## What happens next

SetsOfLove is small, and reports go to a contact address, not an automated system. This post can't promise a particular response time or outcome, and it wouldn't be honest to try.

What can be said is how profiles are handled in general. Every profile is approved by the admin before it goes live, and an edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again. When a profile is rejected or taken down, the member receives a note about what to change. So if a profile breaks the rules, the usual path is a takedown with a note, followed by the member fixing the problem.

You may not hear every detail about what was done. That's partly to protect the privacy of the member involved. Our post on [how profile approval works](/blog/how-profile-approval-works/) describes the approval step in more detail.

## When to go somewhere else as well

A report to SetsOfLove covers a profile on SetsOfLove. Some situations need more.

- **If you're in danger, or someone else is,** call 000.
- **If someone asks you for money or you think you've been scammed,** stop sending anything and see [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/). Report cybercrime through ReportCyber at [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/), and contact your bank straight away if you've paid anything.
- **If you're being harassed, or an image of you was shared without your consent,** the [eSafety Commissioner](https://www.esafety.gov.au/) explains your options and how to report.
- **If someone is bothering you at your gym,** tell the staff, who can act on their own premises.

You can do these in parallel with a report to SetsOfLove. They're not alternatives. Our post on [spotting a fake profile](/blog/how-to-spot-a-fake-profile/) is a useful read if you're unsure about what you've seen.

## If you're the one being reported

If your profile is taken down, you'll get a note on what to change. Read it, fix the issue and send the profile for approval again. Most problems come down to a photo, a text field or a contact detail that shouldn't be there. If you think the decision is wrong, you can reply to the contact address with your reasons, calmly and with specifics.

Nothing here is a judgement on you as a person. Profile rules exist so that everyone, including the person reading your page, feels comfortable.

## Reporting is part of looking after each other

Members decide where to share their link, and introductions happen through friends or at the gym. That works best when everyone can speak up when something looks wrong. If you're the friend who shared a link, you're well placed to notice problems too. Reporting a profile isn't dramatic or unfriendly. It's a simple, useful way to keep the place decent.
