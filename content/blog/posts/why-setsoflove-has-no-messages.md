title: Why SetsOfLove has no messages, comments or algorithm
description: Why this dating app has no messages, comments, likes or algorithm, what that protects, and the honest trade-offs members should know about.
---
SetsOfLove has no messages, comments, likes or algorithm because we wanted a profile page, not a feed. Nobody can send you an unasked remark about your photos, and nothing ranks you against anyone. The cost is real too: it's slower, quieter and depends on you sharing your link.

## What does "no messages" actually mean?

On SetsOfLove, an approved profile is a page at its own link. People who open it can read it and look at the photos. They can't message you, comment on a photo, like it, or be shown it by a system that decides who sees what. It doesn't pair people up either.

Introductions happen the way they do in the rest of life: through friends, or at the gym. You decide where your link goes. Everyone who sees it saw it because you sent it, or someone you trust did.

## Why leave messages out?

This is a choice with reasons behind it, so here they are.

- **No unsolicited remarks.** An inbox invites comments on bodies and photos that no one asked for. With no inbox and no comment box, that channel doesn't exist. For anyone who has had to scroll past those remarks, that's a big part of the point.
- **No pile-up.** Being open to meeting people shouldn't mean opening 40 conversations you didn't want. A page with no message button can't fill up.
- **No performance.** Likes and comments turn a profile into a scoreboard. Without them, a profile is just a plain description of a person.
- **No hidden ranking.** An algorithm decides who gets seen and who doesn't, and you can't see why. Here, nobody is ranked. A profile is visible to people you've shared it with, and that's all.
- **Less pressure to be everything.** When there's no audience measuring you, you can write a profile that sounds like you.

For many members, especially anyone who cares about consent and about being themselves, that last point matters most. Your photos and words are there to describe you. They aren't there to collect reactions.

## What are the honest trade-offs?

Leaving things out has costs, and it's better to say so plainly.

1. **It's slower.** There's no stream of people to browse. If you want a quick reply, this isn't the quickest way.
2. **There's no feedback.** With no likes or views you can act on, you won't know whether anyone has looked at your profile. Silence can be about nothing at all.
3. **It relies on your circle.** If you don't know many people at your gym, or you're new to the city, you have fewer natural places to send your link.
4. **There's no low-pressure first hello inside the app.** For shy people, a message can feel easier than speaking in person. Here, the first hello happens outside the app.
5. **You have to take the first step.** Because nobody can reach you, you may have to pass your link along, or ask a friend to.
6. **It won't suit everyone.** Some people want a large pool and a lot of choice. This isn't that, and we'd rather say so than pretend.

None of these are bugs we're about to fix. They come with the design. If the trade feels wrong for you, that's a fair answer.

## How do people actually meet, then?

Three ordinary routes. A friend you both know says "you two should meet" and sends a link. You get chatting at the gym, in a class or at an event, and later the link makes it easy to find out a bit more. Or you share your link with a group you trust, and someone in it knows a person worth introducing.

If you want help with the sharing side, [how to share your profile link without it being awkward](/blog/how-to-share-your-profile-link/) has wording you can borrow. The reasoning behind meeting through friends is in [why introductions through mutual friends can feel safer](/blog/introductions-through-mutual-friends/). Introductions aren't a guarantee of anything, so the usual safety habits still apply.

## What protections are there instead?

Without messages, the controls sit elsewhere:

- Every profile is approved by the admin before it goes live.
- Contact details such as phone numbers and social handles aren't allowed in public text fields.
- Every public profile has a "Report this profile" link. Reports are covered in [how to report a profile on SetsOfLove](/blog/how-to-report-a-profile/).
- Search engines are told not to list profiles.
- Rejections and takedowns come with a note on what to change.

That isn't the same as saying nothing can go wrong. People can still be unkind outside the app, and no system catches everything. If something feels off, trust that feeling, report it, and step away. For scams and online harm, [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/) and [eSafety](https://www.esafety.gov.au/) are good places to start.

## Is this the right fit for you?

A short decision rule. This design suits you if you'd rather be introduced than approached, you have a gym, class or friend group to share your link with, and you value control over speed. It probably doesn't if you want a large pool of strangers, instant replies or a live conversation before meeting anyone.

Neither answer is wrong. SetsOfLove is free, has no ads, and is for single gym-goers aged 18 or over, of any gender. If a quiet page at your own link sounds useful, it's there. If not, we'd rather you found what works for you.
