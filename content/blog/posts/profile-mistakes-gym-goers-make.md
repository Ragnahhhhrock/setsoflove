The most common dating profile mistakes gym-goers make are a blurry or dark photo, a hidden face, a list of rules and a bio that could belong to anyone. All of them are easy to fix. Here are 9, each with a quick repair, so you can check your profile in about 10 minutes.

## Which photo mistakes cost the most?

Photos do the first job, so start there.

1. **A blurry or dark main photo.** Gym lighting is rough, and a shaky mirror shot can turn you into a smudge. Fix it: clean the lens, stand near a window or a bright patch, and take 5 shots instead of one. Pick the sharpest.
2. **Only one kind of photo.** Four mirror selfies in the same kit tell people one thing about you. Fix it: use up to 4 photos and vary them. One clear face photo, one training shot, and one or two from life outside the gym.
3. **A face you can't see.** A cap, sunglasses, a bad angle or a faraway shot makes it hard to tell who you are. The main photo needs your face clearly visible. Fix it: take it in daylight, no shading, looking roughly at the camera. More on this in [why your main photo should clearly show your face](/blog/main-photo-show-your-face/).

A short note on rules: gym and workout photos are welcome, but bystanders, screenshots, memes and images with text or logos aren't. The full list is in [photo rules: what's welcome and what isn't](/blog/setsoflove-photo-rules/).

## Which writing mistakes put people off?

Words do the second job, and most mistakes here are about tone.

4. **A list of rules or dealbreakers.** "No drama, no games, no time-wasters" tells people what you're tired of, not who you are. Fix it: say what you're after, in positive terms. "I'd like someone who's up for a long walk after a session" beats a list of nos.
5. **Negativity.** Complaints about past experiences or about other people read as heavy before anyone has said hello. Fix it: write about what you enjoy now. Leave the history for later conversations.
6. **A bio that could belong to anyone.** "Love the gym, love travel, love a laugh" describes about half the population. Fix it: add one detail only you could write. A favourite lift, the café you go to after leg day, the thing you're learning. The guide to [how to write a profile bio when you train](/blog/how-to-write-a-profile-bio-when-you-train/) has structures to borrow.

A dry joke is fine here, and one is plenty. If you're stuck, go with what's true over what sounds clever.

## Which detail mistakes are easy to miss?

The smaller fields get less attention, and that's where several slips happen.

7. **Out-of-date photos.** A picture from 3 years ago, or from before a big change in how you look or live, gives people the wrong idea when you meet. Fix it: use recent photos that look like you today. If you're unsure when to refresh them, [how often should you update your profile photos](/blog/how-often-to-update-profile-photos/) gives a simple rule.
8. **Lifts with no context, or numbers that aren't real.** Lifts are optional, and they're self-reported, so they only work if they're honest. A figure you hit once on a good day isn't your working number. Fix it: either leave the lifts out or enter real ones. If you wouldn't say it to someone's face, don't type it.
9. **A vague or half-filled "what you're after".** You can pick any mix of dates, a relationship, marriage, friendship and just fun. Leaving it vague, or choosing everything to be safe, makes your intent hard to read. Fix it: pick what's true today. You can edit later.

One practical warning. Phone numbers and social handles aren't allowed in public text fields, so don't try to tuck one into your bio. It will hold up your approval.

## How do you check your own profile fast?

Use this 5-point pass, in order:

- Is the main photo sharp and clearly you?
- Do the photos show more than one side of you?
- Does the bio say what you're after, positively?
- Is there at least one specific detail about your training and one about life outside it?
- Are the photos and the numbers current and honest?

If you can say yes to all 5, you're ahead of most profiles.

## What should you do after you fix things?

Make all your changes at once. Every profile is approved by the admin before it goes live, and any edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again. If you fix one thing now and another tomorrow, you'll wait twice. Collect the edits, then send the profile for approval a single time.

If something is rejected, you'll get a note on what to change. Read it as help, not criticism. [How profile approval works on SetsOfLove](/blog/how-profile-approval-works/) explains the whole process.

## Is it worth being this careful?

Yes, because small things add up. A clear photo, a warm sentence and an honest number take about 10 minutes, and they make it easier for a friend to share your link with confidence.

You don't need to be someone else, and you don't need to look a certain way. You need a profile that's clear, kind and recognisably you. If yours does that, the rest is up to the people who see it.
