---
Good dating profile privacy comes down to one question: could a stranger use this detail to find or follow you? This 10-point checklist works for any app, site or shared link. Run through it before you publish, and again whenever you change your profile.

## The checklist at a glance

1. Check the background of every photo.
2. Keep location detail broad.
3. Leave out phone numbers and social handles.
4. Be general about your workplace.
5. Do not share your routine.
6. Use only your first name, or a first name and initial.
7. Choose your link and username carefully.
8. Keep other people out of your photos.
9. Decide where you share your link.
10. Know how to report and remove.

The sections below explain each point and what to do about it.

## Points 1 to 3: photos, location and contact details

**1. Check the background of every photo.** Look past yourself. Street signs, house numbers, number plates, school or work uniforms, gym names, logos and distinctive buildings can all identify a place. Photo files can also carry location data, so be aware of what your phone adds when you take a picture. Check your camera settings, or take the photo in a neutral spot such as a plain wall.

**2. Keep location detail broad.** A suburb or city is usually enough for someone to know whether you live near each other. A street, a building or a local cafe you visit every day is not. Describe the area, not the address.

**3. Leave out phone numbers and social handles.** These let anyone find you outside the place you chose to share. Move to other channels only when you've talked for a while, met, and feel comfortable. SetsOfLove doesn't allow contact details in public text fields, and that's a good rule to follow anywhere.

## Points 4 to 6: work, routine and name

**4. Be general about your workplace.** "Nurse", "electrician" or "works in finance" tells people enough. The employer's name, your site or your shift pattern makes you easier to locate.

**5. Do not share your routine.** "Early mornings" is fine. "Monday, Wednesday and Friday at 5:30 am at the gym on the corner" is a schedule. Our post on [keeping your gym and routine private](/blog/keeping-your-gym-and-routine-private/) covers when to share more and how to say no.

**6. Use only your first name, or a first name and initial.** A full name, especially an unusual one, makes it easy to look you up. Share your surname when you've met someone and feel comfortable.

## Points 7 to 8: links, usernames and other people

**7. Choose your link and username carefully.** Names that include a surname, birth year, phone number or workplace say more than you intend. A first name with a number or an initial, such as sam-t, is a good pattern. On SetsOfLove, your link uses lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, 3 to 30 characters.

**8. Keep other people out of your photos.** Friends, partners, children and strangers in the background haven't agreed to be on your profile. Crop them out or choose a different photo. SetsOfLove doesn't allow photos of other people without their consent, and for good reason.

Only post photos you'd be comfortable seeing elsewhere. Anyone who can see a photo can save or copy it.

## Points 9 to 10: sharing, reporting and removing

**9. Decide where you share your link.** Sharing is the part of privacy that you control most directly. Send your link to friends and people you trust, and think before posting it on public pages. On SetsOfLove, search engines are told not to list profiles, and members decide where their link goes, so who sees it is largely up to you. Our posts on [how to share your profile link](/blog/how-to-share-your-profile-link/) and [whether it's safe to share your profile link](/blog/is-it-safe-to-share-your-profile-link/) go through the choices.

**10. Know how to report and remove.** Before you publish anywhere, find out how to report a profile, hide yours or delete it. On SetsOfLove, every public profile has a "Report this profile" link, and any edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again. Check where those options are on any other service too, so you aren't searching when you need them.

## A quick test before you publish

Read your profile as if you were a stranger who wanted to find you. Ask:

- Could I work out where this person lives or trains?
- Could I work out when they're alone?
- Could I contact them without them agreeing?
- Could I find their full name, employer or family?

If the answer to any is yes, edit that detail. Keep the information you share broad, and add more later, in conversation, when you've decided to.

## Habits that matter after you publish

Privacy isn't a one-time job.

- Review your profile every few months and remove details that have crept in.
- Don't send identifying documents, financial details or private images to anyone you've only just met.
- Be careful about public Wi-Fi, shared devices and weak passwords. The [Australian Cyber Security Centre](https://www.cyber.gov.au/) has plain guidance.
- Move slowly with new contacts and use a video call before an in-person meeting. See [online dating safety tips for Australians](/blog/online-dating-safety-tips-for-australians/).
- Trust your instincts. If someone asks for more than you want to give, say no.

If you're asked for money, or something looks like a scam, report it through [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/). Privacy is a series of small, deliberate choices, and each one is yours to make.
