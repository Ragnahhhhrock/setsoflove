Good overhead press form means a firm grip just outside shoulder width, a braced trunk, a stable stance and a bar that moves in a near-straight line past your face. Start light and give your shoulders time to adapt. A qualified coach can check your setup and your shoulder mobility, which is the part most people can't judge alone.

## How do you set up for overhead press form?

The overhead press (sometimes called the shoulder press) takes the bar from your shoulders to above your head while you stand. It works the shoulders and arms and asks your trunk and legs to hold you steady, so any wobble in your setup shows up quickly. Take the same steps each time.

1. **Set the bar at about upper-chest height in the rack.** You want to unrack it without rising on your toes.
2. **Take your grip.** Hands just outside shoulder width is a common starting point. Wrap your thumbs around the bar.
3. **Put the bar on the fronts of your shoulders.** Keep your elbows slightly in front of the bar, with your forearms close to vertical.
4. **Unrack and step back.** Take a small step and set your feet about hip to shoulder width apart.
5. **Brace.** Breathe in low, tighten your middle and squeeze your glutes. Stand tall.

If the bar rests uncomfortably on your collarbone, a small adjustment to your elbow position usually helps. Ask a coach rather than forcing it.

## How do you brace and stand?

A stable base makes the press feel easier. A wobbly one makes it feel heavy.

- **Feet flat, weight in the middle of your foot.** Not on your toes, not on your heels.
- **Squeeze your glutes.** This stops you from leaning back.
- **Brace your trunk before each rep.** Fill your belly and sides with air and hold it as the bar goes up.
- **Ribs down.** Don't let your lower back arch hard. If it does, the weight is probably too heavy, or your shoulders need more mobility.

You should be able to finish a rep without your hips shooting forward.

## What is the right bar path?

The bar needs to get past your face, so your head moves back a little and then forward again.

- **Start with the bar on your shoulders and your chin slightly tucked back.** This clears a path for the bar.
- **Press the bar straight up past your face.** Don't crane your neck to get out of the way.
- **Once the bar passes your forehead, move your head forward through the gap.** Your head ends up just in front of the bar, and the bar finishes over the middle of your body.
- **Finish with your arms straight and the bar above your shoulders and hips.**
- **Lower the bar back to your shoulders with control.** Tuck your chin back again to let it pass.

Keep the bar close. If it drifts out in front, the lift gets harder and your lower back works overtime.

## Does shoulder mobility matter?

Yes. To press overhead comfortably, your shoulders, upper back and the muscles around your ribs need enough range to bring your arms straight above your head. Tight shoulders often show up as an arched lower back, because your body finds the missing range somewhere else.

A simple check is to stand against a wall and raise your arms overhead without your lower back leaving the wall. If you can't do that comfortably, press with lighter weight, try dumbbells or ask a physio or coach for mobility work. Don't force it.

Dumbbells let each arm find its own path, and many people find them more comfortable at first. A seated version, with back support, takes the legs and balance out and can help you learn the movement. Both are common, and neither is a lesser lift.

## What are the common overhead press mistakes?

- **Leaning back to push the bar up.** This moves the load into your lower back.
- **Pressing in front of the body.** The bar should finish over your shoulders, not out ahead of them.
- **Elbows drifting out to the sides.** Keep your forearms close to vertical.
- **Not moving the head out of the way.** Hitting your chin is a clear sign to adjust your path.
- **Loose trunk.** Skip the brace and the lift wobbles.
- **Locking your knees hard.** Keep them soft but straight.
- **Adding weight too fast.** Smaller steps are fine, and it's all progress.
- **Rushing the unrack.** Settle first, then press.

## How should you progress?

Go gradually. Shoulders like steady loading and don't like sudden jumps. Add a small amount only when your last session felt controlled, and go back a step if your form changes. A tiny plate on each side is plenty.

Write down what you did so you can see how things are going over weeks, not days. [How to track your lifts](/blog/how-to-track-your-lifts/) covers the basics. Rest between sessions, and treat a sore day as a cue to take it easy.

## When should you get help?

A qualified coach can watch your press from the side and front, adjust your grip and bar path and tell you whether your shoulders are ready for a barbell. That's worth a session or two, especially for a lift where small errors travel up into the neck and back.

Stop and see a doctor or physio if you feel sharp pain in your shoulder, pain that doesn't ease, or numbness or tingling in your arm or hand. Don't push through it.
