Consent and gym photos come down to one idea: people at the gym are there to train, not to appear in someone else's profile. SetsOfLove doesn't allow photos of other people without their consent, so the best approach is to keep bystanders out of the frame. A little planning gets you a clear photo with just you in it.

## Why gym photos need consent and bystanders stay out

A gym is a shared space where people are focused, often in their least polished moments, and not expecting a camera. Someone in the background of your photo hasn't agreed to be on a dating profile. They may not want their face, their body, their training time or their gym linked to a page that strangers can open.

For some people it's more than a preference. Someone avoiding an ex, someone who's had unwanted attention, or someone who simply values privacy might have good reasons to stay out of photos. You can't see those reasons from across the room, which is why the default is simple: if a person is identifiable and hasn't said yes, they shouldn't be in the picture.

This also protects you. A photo with bystanders can lead to awkward conversations, complaints to staff or a profile being sent back for changes. A clean photo avoids all of that.

## What SetsOfLove allows

Profiles can have 1 to 4 photos, and the main photo shows your face clearly. Gym and workout photos are encouraged. The rules for other people are plain:

- No photos of other people without their consent.
- Nothing sexual and no nudity.
- No screenshots, memes or images with text or logos.

Every profile is approved by the admin before it goes live. If a photo includes someone who shouldn't be there, the profile can be sent back with a note on what to change, and fixing it is usually a matter of picking another shot. The post on [photo rules](/blog/setsoflove-photo-rules/) lists what's welcome in full.

## How to take a mirror selfie without catching others

Mirror selfies are welcome here. A few practical choices keep other people out of them.

1. **Pick your moment.** Quieter times make this easy. Early mornings or the middle of the afternoon often have more space than evenings, though it varies by gym.
2. **Choose your spot.** A mirror in a corner, a stretching area or a hallway gives you control over what's behind you. A big mirror in a busy section tends to catch half the floor.
3. **Check the reflection.** The mirror shows the whole room behind you. Look at the preview before you take the shot, and check the edges.
4. **Frame tightly.** Crop in on yourself, or hold the phone higher or lower so the background is mostly wall.
5. **Wait.** If someone walks into the frame, pause, take a breath and try again in a minute. It takes seconds, and it's courteous.
6. **Check afterwards.** Look at the full-size image, zoomed in, before you upload it.

Our guide to [taking a good mirror selfie for your profile](/blog/mirror-selfie-tips-for-your-profile/) covers the rest, including lighting and kit.

## If someone is in the shot

If a person is partly in the frame, you have three honest options. You can retake the photo. You can crop them out, as long as nothing identifiable is left. Or you can ask them. A quick, friendly "Sorry, I've caught you in the background of a photo, is that okay?" is polite, and it means you can then use the shot or delete it, depending on the answer.

A blurred face or a blocked-out figure isn't a perfect fix. People can often recognise a build, clothing or a tattoo, and the edit can look odd. Retaking the photo is usually simpler.

The same applies to friends. If your training partner is in a photo, ask them first and respect a no, even if you think the picture is great. They get to decide where their image goes.

## Never film or photograph people who haven't agreed

Beyond your own profile, there's a bigger rule: don't film or photograph other gym members in the course of your workout, and don't share images of people without asking. Gyms commonly have their own rules on phones and cameras, so check the policy or ask staff if you're unsure. Changing rooms and similar areas are places where people expect privacy, and a camera doesn't belong there at all.

If you want a video of a lift for form-checking, ask whoever is nearby to stay out of frame, or choose a spot and time where nobody else is around. Most people are happy to help when asked directly. The post on [gym etiquette for single people](/blog/gym-etiquette-for-singles/) covers this and other shared-space habits.

## If someone photographs or films you

If you're photographed or filmed without agreeing, you're allowed to say so. You can ask the person to delete it. If that's uncomfortable, or if they refuse, tell gym staff and ask what they can do. Note the time, what happened and what the person looked like, in case you need it later.

If an image of you has been shared online without your consent, the [eSafety Commissioner](https://www.esafety.gov.au/) can explain your options and how to report it. If you feel unsafe, call 000. It's never your fault, and you don't need to wait until something is serious to speak up.

If you find a SetsOfLove profile with a photo of you that you didn't agree to, use the "Report this profile" link on the page. Say clearly that you're the person in the photo and that you didn't give consent.

## The short version

Keep other people out of your photos. Choose a quiet moment, check your reflection, ask before including anyone, and retake the shot if in doubt. It takes a minute, and it shows the same respect you'd want from a stranger holding a camera.
