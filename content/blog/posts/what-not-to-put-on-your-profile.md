What not to put on a dating profile comes down to anything that lets a stranger find you in person before you've chosen to meet: contact details, your full surname, workplace specifics, your daily routine and photos of other people. You can still write a warm, specific profile without any of it. The aim is to be easy to like and hard to track.

## Start with a simple test

Before you add a detail, ask: "Would I tell this to someone I'd met once, in a queue?" If yes, it's probably fine. If you'd hesitate, leave it for later, when you know the person better.

This isn't about hiding who you are. A profile can be honest, personal and distinctly yours while keeping back details that only matter once trust exists. You decide what to share, and you can share more over time. Nobody is owed information about you just because they've read your page.

## What not to put on a dating profile: the details to leave out

| Leave out | Why | Say this instead |
|---|---|---|
| Phone number, email, social handles | Anyone who sees the page can contact you, and you can't choose who | Nothing. You can share contact details with someone once you've decided to |
| Full surname | Makes you easy to look up across other sites | First name only |
| Workplace name, role and roster | Tells people where to find you and when | "I work in healthcare" or "I work shifts" |
| Exact gym, class times and weekly schedule | Tells people where you'll be at a set time | "I train early mornings" |
| Home street, building or a photo that shows it | Reveals where you live | Your suburb, if you're comfortable |
| Number plates, uniforms with names, name tags | Small details that identify you | Crop or choose another photo |

SetsOfLove doesn't allow contact details such as phone numbers and social handles in public text fields, which handles the first row for you. The rest are your call.

## Your routine is the detail people forget

A profile often says when and where you train without meaning to. "At the gym at 5:30 am, Mon, Wed, Fri" gives a stranger a timetable. "Early starts suit me" doesn't.

Use general terms: "mornings", "after work", "weekends". Keep the name of your gym out of the text. If you do want to tell someone which gym you use, wait until after you've met in a public place and felt comfortable. Our post on [keeping your gym and routine private when you date](/blog/keeping-your-gym-and-routine-private/) goes further on this.

The same goes for travel. "Away all next week" is information for anyone who might want to know when your home is empty. Save it for people you know.

## Photos can say more than you intend

A photo is easy to treat as just a face, but backgrounds carry plenty of detail. Before you upload, look at the edges of each one.

- Check for street signs, house numbers, a car's number plate, a workplace pass or a uniform badge.
- Look at the mirror. A mirror selfie can show your hallway, your bedroom or the person behind you.
- Check the gym floor. Logos, name tags and other people's faces are all in the background of many gym shots.
- Skip photos of other people. SetsOfLove doesn't allow photos of other people without their consent, and the main photo should show your face clearly. Our post on [consent and gym photos](/blog/consent-and-gym-photos/) explains why this matters.

Photos with text, logos, memes and screenshots aren't allowed either, and nudity or anything sexual is out. The [photo rules](/blog/setsoflove-photo-rules/) list what's welcome. Gym and workout photos are encouraged, so you've got plenty to choose from without giving away your address.

## Be careful with personal stories

Some details are hard to take back once they're public: a recent breakup in detail, a medical history, your finances, details about your kids or family, or a former partner's name. You can mention that you're a parent or recovering from an injury if you want to, but you don't have to, and the level of detail is yours to choose.

Gender and who you'd like to meet are part of a SetsOfLove profile, and they're shown as you choose them. Beyond those fields, nothing requires you to explain your identity, history or body. Write what feels right to you and leave out what doesn't. If anyone ever pressures you for more, you can say no.

Also leave out anything that could hurt other people or invite conflict: rants, political arguments, a list of things you can't stand. Positive framing, saying what you're after, works better and keeps the page welcoming.

## A short pre-publish check

Before you send a profile for approval, read it once as a stranger would.

1. Could someone work out where I live, work or train at a set time?
2. Is there any phone number, handle or email?
3. Does my link name use my full surname? Link names can be lowercase letters, numbers and hyphens, 3 to 30 characters, so something like a first name and an initial works.
4. Is every person in every photo someone who agreed to be there?
5. Is anything here that I'd regret seeing printed in a group chat?

Remember that any edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again, so it pays to batch your changes. If you spot a profile that shows someone's contact details or uses a person's photos without consent, you can use its "Report this profile" link.
