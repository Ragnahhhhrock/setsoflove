To convert kg to lb, multiply by 2.20462. To convert lb to kg, divide by 2.20462. So a 100 kg lift is 220.5 lb, and a 225 lb lift is 102.1 kg. The tables below cover the plate and bar weights you'll see most often, so you can do a gym conversion in your head.

## What is the formula for kg to lb?

One kilogram equals 2.20462 pounds. That's the whole formula.

**lb = kg x 2.20462**

Worked example: a bar loaded to 80 kg. Multiply 80 by 2.20462 and you get 176.3696. Rounded to 1 decimal place, that's 176.4 lb.

If you want a quick estimate without a calculator, double the kg figure and add 10 per cent of the result. For 80 kg, double is 160, and 10 per cent of 160 is 16, so you get 176. It lands very close.

Bars and plates vary between gyms, so check the weight stamped on the equipment if you're unsure. The bar counts toward the total, so a 20 kg bar with a 20 kg plate on each side is 60 kg, not 40 kg.

## How do you convert lb back to kg?

Divide by 2.20462. If you'd rather multiply, use 0.45359, which gives almost the same answer.

**kg = lb / 2.20462**

Notice that a 45 lb bar is 20.4 kg, not exactly 20. Those 2 bars are close, but they aren't identical. If you log lifts in both units, expect small differences.

## How should you round?

Round for the job you're doing.

- **When you're loading a bar,** use the plates you actually have. If your target is 176.4 lb and your gym has kg plates, you load 80 kg and call it done.
- **When you're writing it down,** pick 1 unit and stick with it. Convert for the sake of comparison, but don't keep switching.
- **When you're sharing it,** 1 decimal place is plenty. 220.5 lb is fine. You don't need 220.462.
- **When two numbers look different,** it's probably rounding. 100 kg could be written as 220 lb or 220.5 lb, and both are fair.

One trap to avoid is converting your lifts back and forth several times. Each round-trip can drift by a fraction. Convert once from the original number, and keep the original.

## Tips for keeping your numbers straight

- **Record the unit your gym uses.** If the plates say kg, write kg. It saves you doing sums mid-session.
- **Write the unit every time.** A bare "100" could mean either.
- **Keep the bar weight in mind.** Note whether your total includes it.
- **Don't change units to feel better about a number.** 100 kg and 220.5 lb are the same lift.
- **Compare like with like.** If you read about a lift online, check the unit first.

If you're travelling, you may land in a gym that uses the other unit. Take a screenshot of the tables above, or learn the 2 anchors: 20 kg is about 44 lb and 45 lb is about 20 kg.
