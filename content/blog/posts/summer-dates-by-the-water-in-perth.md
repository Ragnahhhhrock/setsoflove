The best summer date ideas in Perth start early or end late. Beach mornings, a swim before the day warms up, a sunset walk along the river or coast and a long stretch in the shade all work well, as long as you plan for sun, wind and water conditions.

## Why do early starts suit summer dates?

Perth summers are bright and strong. By midday the sun is intense, the sand is uncomfortable and the car parks are busy. Early in the morning, the light is softer, the water is calmer and the whole place has a quiet feel that makes conversation easy.

An early start also gives the date a natural end. You meet, walk, swim or have a coffee, and then the day carries on. Nobody has to explain why they're leaving after an hour.

If you are new to the city, or new to each other, this is a gentle way to spend time together. It's also a good fit if you train in the morning anyway and want to keep your routine intact.

## A beach morning, step by step

Here is a simple shape that works for most people:

1. Meet at a spot you've both agreed on, in daylight, with a clear place to find each other.
2. Walk along the sand or the path beside it first. Talk, look around, see how it feels.
3. If you both want to swim, go in together and stay where you can stand or where the beach looks well patrolled.
4. Dry off, find a bench or some shade and have a coffee or a snack.
5. Decide whether to keep going or call it a good morning.

Perth has plenty of beaches, from long open stretches to calmer, sheltered bays. Conditions change with the wind, the swell and the time of year, so check the current forecast and any posted warnings before you go. If a beach has flags and patrols, swim between the flags. If you are unsure about the surf, stay at the water's edge. Nobody is judging your swimming.

## Swims, paddles and the river

You don't need the ocean for a water date. The Swan River offers calmer water, plenty of paths and places to sit. A paddle on the river or a gentle walk along the foreshore can be just as enjoyable as a beach trip, and it's easier if either of you is nervous about waves.

If you are trying something new, like a kayak or a paddleboard, treat the first go as a learning session. Check the weather, wear a life jacket where it's recommended and let someone know your plan. ## Sunset walks

If mornings aren't your thing, sunsets are the other sweet spot. The heat eases, the colours are good and the mood is relaxed. A walk along the foreshore or the sand as the light fades is simple and can be as short or as long as you like.

Two practical points. First, daylight disappears faster than people expect, so plan your walk to finish while you can still see where you are going. Second, getting home in the dark is part of the plan. Know how you'll travel, keep your phone charged and think about how each of you will get back.

## Sun safety is part of the date

A sunburn is a bad souvenir. A few habits cover most of it:

- Apply sunscreen before you leave and again after swimming or sweating.
- Wear a hat and sunglasses.
- Choose shade for breaks, especially in the middle of the day.
- Carry water for both of you, or at least offer to share.
- Cover up if you notice your skin getting pink. It's quicker to move into shade than to recover afterwards.

If you've had skin concerns or you are unsure about your own sun risk, a doctor can give advice that fits you. For tips on keeping your own training safe in warm weather, see [training in an Australian summer](/blog/training-in-an-australian-summer/).

## Pack light, plan a backup

You don't need much for a water date:

- Towel and a change of clothes
- Water bottle
- Sunscreen, hat and sunglasses
- A small bag you can keep an eye on
- Phone, charged, with a friend's number handy

Keep valuables to a minimum, and don't leave bags unattended on the sand while you swim. Have a backup in mind in case the wind picks up or the forecast changes. A shaded cafe terrace, a walk in a nearby park or simply an earlier finish are all fine options.

## Look after each other, and yourself

New people are new people, even at a beach. Meet in a public place, make your own way there and home, and tell a friend where you'll be and when you expect to be done. Send a quick message after you finish so they know you are okay.

On the date itself, check in. Ask whether the sun is getting too much, offer a drink, and be relaxed if someone wants to skip the swim or leave early. Respect works the same way on the sand as anywhere else. If someone says no to a swim or a photo, the answer is no, and the date carries on.

Summer dates by the water suit people who like to move, but they also suit people who like to sit and talk. If you are not sure which type your date is, ask. It's a lot easier to plan a day when you both know what you're signing up for. And if the plan changes, you can always head for the shade and a cold drink.
