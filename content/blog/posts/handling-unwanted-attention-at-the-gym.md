---
title: Handling unwanted attention at the gym
description: What to do about unwanted attention at the gym: short firm lines, moving away, telling staff, keeping notes and finding support. It's never your fault.
---
If you're getting unwanted attention at the gym, you can say a short, clear no, move away, and tell a staff member. It is never your fault, whatever you wear, how you train or how friendly you've been. You don't have to be polite at the expense of your comfort, and you don't have to put up with it quietly.

## It is never your fault

Unwanted attention can be staring, comments about your body, repeated attempts to start a conversation, standing too close, following you between areas or turning up whenever you train. Whatever form it takes, the responsibility sits with the person doing it.

You didn't invite it by being friendly, by wearing training clothes, by taking a mirror selfie or by being good at something. A gym is a place to train. You are entitled to do that without being approached, watched or commented on.

If it's making you uncomfortable, that's enough reason to act. You don't need to prove it's serious first.

## Short, firm lines you can use

You don't need a speech. Short, calm sentences are harder to argue with. Say it once, face the person if you feel safe doing so, and keep your tone level.

- "I'm not interested in chatting. Thanks."
- "Please don't comment on my body."
- "I'd like to train in peace."
- "I've said no. Please leave me alone."
- "Please step back."

You can skip the explanation. "No" is a complete sentence, and you don't owe anyone a reason, a smile or a softer version. If the person keeps going, repeat the same line without adding anything. Changing your words each time gives them something to respond to.

If saying something feels unsafe, don't. Choosing to move away or find a staff member instead is a perfectly good response.

## Move, and change what you can

Your first move is often physical. You can:

- Walk to a busier part of the gym or closer to the front desk.
- Put in headphones and turn away.
- Pick up your gear and switch equipment.
- Go to a group class or ask a friend to join you.
- Leave for the day if you want to. That is a valid decision, not a defeat.

Changing your session time or your usual spot is also fine if it helps. It isn't giving in. It's a short-term step that protects your comfort while you deal with the situation, and you can go back to your normal routine later. Nobody should have to change their routine permanently, and the aim is for them to be the one who changes.

## Tell the gym staff

Staff can only help if they know. Tell someone at the front desk, a trainer or a manager, in person or in writing. Gyms usually have policies about member behaviour, and staff can speak to the person, keep an eye on the area, or take further steps under their own rules.

When you speak to them:

1. Say what's happening in plain terms, such as "A member keeps approaching me and commenting on me after I've asked them to stop."
2. Say what you'd like, such as a word with that person, a staff member nearby or a change in their access.
3. Ask what happens next and who your contact is.
4. Ask them to note your report.

You can bring a friend with you for support. If the first person you speak to doesn't take it seriously, ask for the manager. Our post on [gym etiquette for single people](/blog/gym-etiquette-for-singles/) covers the kind of behaviour most gyms expect from everyone.

## Keep a simple record

If it happens more than once, write it down. A short note on your phone is enough.

- The date and time
- Where in the gym it happened
- What was said or done
- Who else saw it, including staff
- Who you told and when

A record helps staff act and helps you remember details if you need them later. If there are cameras, you can ask staff whether footage exists and how to request it. Don't confront the person to collect evidence. Your safety comes first.

## Support and when to get more help

You don't have to handle this alone. Talk to a friend or someone in your family, and let them know what's happening. If the behaviour involves threats, touching you without your consent, or following you outside the gym, treat it as serious. In an emergency or if you feel in immediate danger, call 000.

For confidential support and information in Australia, [1800RESPECT](https://www.1800respect.org.au/) is available. Your doctor or a counsellor can also help if what happened is staying with you.

If the person is someone you know, like a former partner, our post on [seeing an ex at your gym](/blog/seeing-an-ex-at-your-gym/) has options for that situation. For ways of keeping your details to yourself, see [keeping your gym and routine private](/blog/keeping-your-gym-and-routine-private/).

## If it started with a profile or a link

If the attention comes from someone who found you through a dating profile, you can stop all contact, and you don't need their agreement. On SetsOfLove, there are no messages, so nobody can send you messages through the app. Every public profile has a "Report this profile" link, and a profile that's causing problems can be reported. If you'd rather not have a profile of your own online, you can delete it.

Wherever it started, you set the terms. The person behaving badly is the one who has to change.
