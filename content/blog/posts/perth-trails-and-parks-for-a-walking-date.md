A walking date on Perth's trails and parks works because it's simple. You move at the same pace, you have plenty to look at and you can stop whenever you like. Pick a well-used route, bring water, tell a friend your plan and check current conditions before you leave.

## Why does a walking date work so well?

Sitting across a table can feel like an interview. Walking side by side takes the pressure off. You can look at the scenery when you run out of things to say, and silence feels natural rather than awkward.

It also suits people whose lives revolve around training. You likely already know what a comfortable pace feels like, and you don't need to dress up. If you want a plan that is gentle and low-cost, this is one of the best.

The key is choosing the right place. A route that suits a first date is not the same as a route you'd pick for a long weekend hike.

## Parks and gardens: the easy option

For most dates, a park beats a trail. Perth has plenty of parks with paved or well-formed paths, shade trees, seating and public toilets. Kings Park is a well-known example, with bushland, gardens and lookouts in one place. The Swan River foreshore is another reliable choice, with a long run of paths and benches.

Why start with a park?

- Other people are around, which helps with safety and comfort.
- Paths are clear, so you won't get lost.
- You can shorten or lengthen the walk easily.
- Mobile reception is usually good.

If one of you uses a mobility aid or has an injury, ask about accessibility before you pick. Most people are glad to be asked, and the question shows you care about making it enjoyable.

## Bushland and longer trails

Perth is surrounded by bushland and regional parks, and many of them have marked walking trails. These can be wonderful, but they ask more of you. Before committing to a trail on a date, think about:

1. How long will it take, and are you both happy to commit that time?
2. Is the track well marked and popular enough that you'll see other walkers?
3. Is there shade, or will you be in the open sun?
4. What is the phone reception like?
5. Are there any closures, fire restrictions or track works?

Trail conditions change with the season, with weather and with fire risk. Always look up the latest notices from the land manager before you leave, and be ready to switch to a different route if something is closed. If a track feels too remote for a first meeting, save it for later. A busier route is the better first-date choice.

## What to bring

You don't need heavy gear for a short walk, but a few basics matter:

- Water for each of you, more than you think you need
- Sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses
- Sturdy, comfortable shoes
- A small snack to share
- A charged phone

In warmer months, aim for early morning or late afternoon, and avoid the middle of the day. If you want more on heat and training safely, [training in an Australian summer](/blog/training-in-an-australian-summer/) covers the basics. Pack a light jacket in cooler months too, since the weather can change quickly.

## Tell a friend, every time

Telling someone your plan takes a minute and makes a real difference. Before you go, send a friend a message with:

- Where you're meeting
- Who you're meeting (a first name is enough)
- Roughly when you expect to finish

Then check in once you're done. If you are walking in a more remote area, also share which trail you've chosen and when you expect to be back. Keep your phone on, and consider sharing your live location with that friend for the duration.

For a longer list of habits, see [a first date safety checklist](/blog/first-date-safety-checklist/). It covers public places, your own transport, trusting your gut and leaving if something feels wrong.

## How to run the date

Aim to meet at a visible, public starting point such as a car park, entrance or cafe, and travel there independently. Say hello, check that the plan still suits you both and agree on a rough route. A line like "Shall we do the loop to the lookout and see how we feel?" gives you both room.

Along the way:

- Stop for water and photos at natural pauses.
- Ask whether the pace works. Don't assume.
- Keep to the path and look after the park. Take your rubbish with you.
- Be ready to finish early if someone's tired, overheated or uneasy. Plain, friendly honesty is fine.

Stay respectful of other people and the environment, and don't feed or approach wildlife.

## Finishing well

A walk gives you a natural finish line. When you get back to the start, you can say goodbye, grab a coffee or extend the plan. If you want ideas for the next step, [Perth first dates: coffee, a walk and nothing fancy](/blog/perth-first-dates-coffee-a-walk-and-nothing-fancy/) covers the easy combination of a drink and a stroll.

Remember that how it feels matters more than how far you went. A short, relaxed walk that leaves you both with a smile beats a long slog every time.

Check conditions, tell a friend, bring water, wear sunscreen and pick a route that suits you both. After that, the only thing left to do is enjoy the walk.
