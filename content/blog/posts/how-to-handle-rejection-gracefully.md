---
title: How to handle rejection gracefully
description: Learn how to handle rejection with a calm reply, some space and a few kind habits, so a no stays a small moment and you can try again later.
---
To handle rejection well, accept the answer without arguing, reply briefly and kindly, then give yourself some space. A no is information about fit and timing. It is not a verdict on you. The goal is to leave the other person comfortable and to look after yourself.

## What a no actually tells you

When someone says no, or doesn't reply, it feels personal. It usually isn't. People decline for reasons you can't see: they're busy, they're seeing someone, they want something different or they just don't feel it.

Try to keep the facts separate from the story. The fact is, "They said no." The story is, "I'm not good enough." Only the first one is true.

A no also saves you time. You find out early, rather than later, that this wasn't the right fit. That's useful, even when it stings.

## The first minute: what to say

How you respond in the first moment matters most, for you and for them. Keep it short, warm and final.

- "No worries. Thanks for being straight with me."
- "All good. Enjoy your session."
- "Understood. Take care."

Then stop. Don't ask why, don't offer a counter-argument and don't say "maybe another time" unless they have. If you feel your face going red, a simple "right, back to it" gets you out gracefully.

If they have said no by text or in a message, a one-line reply is enough. A long response asks them to take care of your feelings, which isn't fair to them.

What to avoid:

1. **Pushing back.** "Are you sure?" puts pressure on them to explain or reassure you.
2. **Sulking or going cold.** Being short with someone you'll see again makes things harder for both of you.
3. **Asking a third person to change their mind.** Leave it alone.
4. **Turning up where they'll be.** Don't alter your training times to run into them. If you share a gym, return to your usual routine, and see [what to do when someone at the gym isn't interested](/blog/when-someone-at-the-gym-isnt-interested/).

## Take space, then look after yourself

After the moment passes, you may feel embarrassed, flat or a bit angry. All normal. Give those feelings a little room without feeding them.

Some things that help:

- **Move.** A walk or an easy session can clear your head. Don't use training to punish yourself.
- **Tell one person.** A friend who'll listen without turning it into a project is worth a lot.
- **Write it down.** Two lines on what happened and what you'd do the same next time. Then close the notebook.
- **Keep your routine.** Sleep, food and regular training steady you.
- **Hold off on big decisions.** Don't delete your profile or change your whole approach on the same night.

If rejection keeps hitting hard, or you feel low for more than a couple of weeks, talk to your doctor or a counsellor. [Beyond Blue](https://www.beyondblue.org.au/) has information and support.

## Reframe what just happened

A few ways of thinking that help, with a bit of honesty about each:

- **You were brave.** Asking, or putting yourself forward, takes nerve. That's worth recognising whatever the answer.
- **You learned something.** Maybe about timing, how you asked or what you want. Take one lesson, not ten.
- **They did you a favour by being clear.** A clear no is kinder than a vague maybe.
- **It doesn't predict anything.** One no tells you nothing about the next person.

What doesn't help is rewriting it as a story where you were robbed, or where they were cruel. Most people who say no are trying to be decent about it.

## Be the kind of person who takes a no well

How you handle rejection is also how people come to know you. A gracious response often leaves a better impression than a yes would have.

It also makes the gym more comfortable for everyone. If you'd like a plan that builds in a way out from the start, [a low-pressure plan for a gym crush](/blog/gym-crush-low-pressure-plan/) shows how to keep things light and know when to stop.

And when you're the one saying no, be kind in return. Be clear and brief, and thank them for asking. "Thanks for asking, but I'm not interested in dating. I hope you have a good session" is enough. You don't owe anyone a long explanation.

## Try again, when you're ready

Trying again later is a good sign, not a sign of desperation. There's no set waiting time. A useful test is whether you can think about the last no without a knot in your stomach.

When you're ready, a few ideas:

- Start with a low-stakes step, like a coffee with a friend of a friend.
- Look at what you want. [Knowing what you're after](/blog/what-are-you-looking-for/) makes it easier to look in the right places.
- Rework your profile if it feels stale. Update photos and wording in one go, because on SetsOfLove an edit takes a live profile offline until it's approved again.
- Remember that members decide where to share their link, so you choose the pace. There's no messaging or likes, which means there's no public tally of who did or didn't respond.

## A short checklist

1. Accept the answer. Don't argue.
2. Reply in one line, kindly.
3. Leave them to their workout.
4. Move, sleep, eat, and talk to someone you trust.
5. Take one lesson and let the rest go.
6. Try again when you're ready, at a pace that suits you.

Rejection stings for a while and then it doesn't. What lasts is how you handled it.
