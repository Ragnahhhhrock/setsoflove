---
title: How to write a profile bio when you train
description: A dating profile bio for fitness people: use your own voice, pick specifics over clichés, add one training detail and one from life outside it.
---
A good dating profile bio for someone who trains is short, specific and sounds like you talking. Include one detail about your training and one about life outside it, then say plainly what you're after. Aim for 3 to 5 sentences, not a manifesto.

## Start with your voice, not a template

Read your draft out loud. If you'd never say it to a friend over coffee, rewrite it. Phrases like "love to laugh" and "work hard, play hard" are everywhere, and they tell a reader nothing.

Write the way you'd explain your week to someone you've just met. Plain words, short sentences, a bit of dry humour if that's you. On SetsOfLove, the "about you" field is your own voice, and members can edit anything the quick picks suggest. Use the quick picks as a nudge, then change them until they're yours.

## Specifics beat clichés

A cliché could be on anyone's profile. A specific could only be on yours.

Instead of "I love fitness", try "I'm in the weights room by 5:45 am, 3 days a week, and asleep by 9:30." Instead of "I love travel", try "I'm saving for a month walking in Tasmania, and I'm very serious about trail snacks."

A quick test: could 1,000 other people paste your sentence onto their own profile? If so, add a detail. A place, a time, a food, a half-finished project or a small strong opinion will do it.

## One detail about training, one about life outside it

This is the structure that does most of the work.

- **One training detail.** What you do and how it fits your week. "I lift in the morning before shifts" tells someone your schedule and your priorities in a single line. If you're time-poor, say so. It saves explaining it later.
- **One detail from outside the gym.** Cooking, a footy team, a dog, a garden, a band you won't shut up about. This shows that training is part of your life, not all of it.

Together they say: I take this seriously, and I'm more than it. That's an easy person to start a conversation with.

## Say what you're after, in positive words

Your bio is a good place to be clear. Say what you want rather than what you can't stand. "Looking for someone who likes a slow Saturday and an early Sunday walk" works better than a list of dealbreakers.

If you want a relationship, say so in a sentence. If you want dates and friendship first, say that. SetsOfLove has fields for what you're after and who you'd like to meet, so the bio doesn't need to repeat them. It can add the colour.

For longer thoughts on that last field, see [how to write the "who you'd like to meet" section](/blog/writing-who-you-would-like-to-meet/).

## A worked example

Here's a made-up draft, then a revision.

*First draft:* "I'm a fitness lover who works hard and plays hard. Looking for someone who shares my passion. No drama, no games."

It's fine, but it could belong to anyone, and half of it describes what you don't want.

*Revision:* "I'm up at 5 am most days and training by 5:30, so my evenings are quiet and my bedtime is early. Outside the gym, I'm usually cooking too much pasta or fixing something on a Saturday. I'd like to meet someone who is happy with a coffee date at 9 am and a relationship that has room for both our routines."

The revision is 3 sentences, contains a real schedule, one life detail and a clear aim. It also tells a reader what a first date could look like. If your week has an odd shape, [dating when you train at 5 am](/blog/dating-when-you-train-at-5am/) explains how to talk about it.

## What to leave out

Some things don't belong in public text:

- Phone numbers, social handles and other contact details. SetsOfLove doesn't allow them in public fields.
- Your exact daily schedule, workplace or full surname. Say "mornings" rather than "the 6:15 class at the corner gym".
- Rants, politics and anything about an ex.
- Lists of rules for whoever reads it.

Keeping a little back is sensible. Plenty will come out in conversation, once you've met. A bio is an invitation to talk, not a full biography, so leave some questions unanswered on purpose.

If you're short on time, write the bio in 2 passes. Spend 10 minutes getting a rough version down, then come back the next day and trim it. Fresh eyes spot filler quickly, and a line that still sounds good after a night's sleep is usually a keeper.

## A quick edit pass

Before you send the profile for approval, run through this list.

1. Does it sound like you, read out loud?
2. Is there one training detail and one detail from outside it?
3. Is every sentence under about 20 words?
4. Does it say what you're after, in positive terms?
5. Are there any contact details or very specific locations?

If you're still stuck on the first line, ["about you" ideas when you're stuck for words](/blog/about-you-ideas/) has prompts and a few weak-to-strong rewrites. Remember that any edit to a live profile takes it offline until it is approved again, so finish your edits before you send.
