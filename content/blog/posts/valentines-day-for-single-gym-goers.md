title: Valentine's Day for single gym-goers
description: Single on Valentine's Day? Here are low-pressure ways to enjoy 14 Feb with friends and a good session, plus a gentle nudge to sort your profile early.
---
Single on Valentine's Day? You can treat 14 Feb as an ordinary day, a day for your friends or a day you plan around something you enjoy. There's no rule that says you should be coupled by then. This year it falls on a Sunday, which makes it easy to build a good day around a decent session and some company.

## You don't have to feel anything in particular

Plenty of people feel fine about the day. Some feel a little flat. Some feel annoyed at the amount of pink in the shops. All of that is normal.

You don't need to prove you're happy, and you don't need to fix your relationship status by the 14th. Dates on a calendar are poor deadlines for something as personal as meeting someone.

A better question is, "What would make this a good day for me?" Then plan that.

## Ideas that suit people who train

Because 14 Feb is a Sunday, the whole day is open. A few ideas, from quiet to social:

1. **A long, unhurried session.** Sunday mornings at the gym are often calmer. Try something you've been putting off, like a new class or a longer warm-up.
2. **A walk or swim with a friend.** Plan it for the morning to beat the heat, and bring water and sun protection.
3. **A Saturday parkrun, then breakfast.** If you'd like something the day before, [parkrun](https://www.parkrun.com.au/) runs free weekly events, and a post-run coffee is an easy plan.
4. **A cook-up at home.** Invite 3 or 4 friends and cook together. Everyone brings one thing.
5. **A rest day, done properly.** Sleep in, read, stretch. Resting well is part of training.
6. **Something for someone else.** Call a family member, or help a friend move house. It's a good way to spend an afternoon.

If you're after more gentle ideas, [rest day date ideas](/blog/rest-day-date-ideas/) works just as well for friends as for dates.

## Make a plan with friends

If you'd like company, ask early. Many people feel the same way and would welcome an invitation. A simple message does the job:

- "A few of us are doing a walk and brunch on the 14th. Want to join?"
- "Anyone fancy a Sunday session and coffee afterwards?"
- "I'm cooking on Valentine's Day. Come over if you're free."

You don't need a theme. You also don't need to explain yourself. "Doing something with friends" is a complete reason.

If you're the person who brings people together, you could also take the chance to introduce two friends who might get on. Check that both are open to it first, keep it light and step back. [How to introduce two friends who both train](/blog/how-to-introduce-friends-who-train/) covers the steps.

## Keep it low-pressure if you do want to meet someone

Maybe you'd like to meet someone, and the day has made you think about it. That's fine. Just don't let a date on the calendar rush you.

A few honest pointers:

- **Don't ask someone out because of the date.** A "better late than lonely" invitation puts pressure on both of you.
- **Don't change your training times to chase someone.** Keep your usual routine.
- **Start with a friendly hello.** If you've been meaning to chat with a familiar face at the gym, a short, easy conversation is plenty. [A low-pressure plan for a gym crush](/blog/gym-crush-low-pressure-plan/) lays out a calm way to do it.
- **Accept the answer you get.** A no, or a quiet reply, is a complete answer.

Meeting someone because of a date on the calendar is a bit like starting a program because it's January. It can work, but it works better when you've thought about what you want.

## If you want to make a profile, do it early

Here's a practical point, which you can ignore if it isn't for you. If you'd like a profile on SetsOfLove, set one up well ahead of 14 Feb, not on the day. Every profile is approved before it goes live, and any edit takes it offline until it's approved again. Allow a bit of time.

A few notes on what a profile involves:

- 1 to 4 photos. The main one shows your face clearly, and gym and workout photos are welcome.
- A few short fields: your name, age, what you do, how you train, and a few words about you.
- You choose who you'd like to meet and what you're after, from dates, a relationship, marriage, friendship or just fun.
- Your profile lives at its own link, and you decide where to share it. There's no messaging.

A profile doesn't promise anything. It's a clear, honest introduction that you can share when you're ready. For help with the writing, see [how to write a profile bio when you train](/blog/how-to-write-a-profile-bio-when-you-train/).

## If the day still feels tough

Some years it just does. If you're feeling low, reach out to a friend or family member, and keep your routine going. Movement, sleep and company help. If the low feeling lingers, speak to your doctor or call [Beyond Blue](https://www.beyondblue.org.au/) for support.

A simple plan for the week of the 14th:

1. Decide what you'd like the day to look like, and tell someone.
2. Book the session, or the walk, or the dinner.
3. Skip the social media scroll if it makes you feel worse.
4. Do one kind thing for yourself.
5. On the 15th, carry on as usual.

It's a Sunday. You can have a good one.
