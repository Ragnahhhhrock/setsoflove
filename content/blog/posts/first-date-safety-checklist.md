---
title: A first date safety checklist
description: A first date safety checklist for before, during and after: choose a public place, tell a friend, charge your phone and trust your gut.
---
A good first date safety checklist is short: pick a public place in daylight or early evening, tell a friend the details, charge your phone, get yourself there and back, and leave if anything feels wrong. These steps take about 10 minutes to set up and barely change the date itself. Here they are in order, so you can run through them the same way each time.

## Before the date

Most of the safety work happens before you leave home. If your week is full of early starts and shifts, do it the day before so it doesn't become one more thing to remember.

1. **Choose the place and time.** Suggest somewhere public with other people around, such as a cafe or a busy park. A coffee or a walk also keeps the date short and easy to end. Earlier in the day is a good default.
2. **Confirm with a quick call or message.** A short chat on the day, or a video call earlier in the week, shows the person is real and still keen.
3. **Tell a friend.** Share the person's name, any profile link or photos you have, the venue, and when you expect to be home. Agree a time you'll check in.
4. **Plan your transport.** Know how you're getting there and back. Check the last train or bus, or have fare and a booked ride as a back-up.
5. **Charge your phone and carry a battery pack** if you have one. Make sure location sharing with your friend works.
6. **Keep personal details back.** You don't need to share your address, workplace or training schedule yet. The post on [keeping your gym and routine private when you date](/blog/keeping-your-gym-and-routine-private/) explains what to hold back and when to share more.

## During the date

You're there to enjoy yourself, and a few habits sit quietly in the background.

- **Stay in the public space.** If the plan changes to a private location, such as someone's home or car, it's fine to say no. "I'd rather stay here" doesn't need a reason.
- **Keep an eye on your drink and your belongings.** Order your own, keep it in sight, and don't leave it with a stranger. If you leave it unattended, get a fresh one.
- **Go easy on alcohol.** Everyone has a different limit. Staying clear-headed keeps your choices yours.
- **Use your own transport.** Say thanks, but no, to a lift, even if it seems friendly. You can accept one on a later date, once you know the person better.
- **Notice how you feel.** Does the person listen when you set a small limit, such as "I can only stay for an hour"? Respect for small boundaries is a good sign. [Setting boundaries early](/blog/setting-boundaries-early-when-you-start-dating/) has simple phrases you can borrow.
- **Check in with your friend** if you said you would, even with a quick thumbs up.

## How to leave if you want to

You don't need a reason. A date is allowed to end early, and you don't owe anyone an explanation beyond basic courtesy. Some options, from gentle to firm:

- "It's been lovely, but I'm going to head off now."
- "I've got an early start tomorrow, so I'll say goodnight here."
- "This isn't working for me. Take care."

If someone pushes back, repeats the question or blocks your way, it's time to move to a busy spot, speak to venue staff, or call a friend or 000 if you feel unsafe. Venue staff can often help if you ask, and you're allowed to ask.

## Trust your gut, and use a simple rule

Feelings are data. If your stomach drops, don't talk yourself out of it. A useful decision rule: if you'd advise a friend to leave in this situation, then leave. You don't need to prove anything went wrong, and you don't need to be polite at the cost of your comfort.

Some things worth noticing, without jumping to conclusions:

- Their story differs from what they told you before, in a way that's hard to explain.
- They look noticeably different from their photos, or avoid questions about it.
- They get angry or sulky when you say no to something small.
- They ask for money, or for details such as your address, early on. If that happens, read our guide to [romance scam warning signs](/blog/romance-scam-warning-signs/).

One odd moment isn't a verdict. A pattern, or a moment that feels bigger than the facts, is your cue to step back.

## After the date

Close the loop with your friend so they know you're home and okay. Then take a few minutes for yourself.

- Ask yourself how you felt during the date, not only how the person seemed. Did you relax? Did you feel listened to?
- If you want to see them again, say so kindly. If you don't, a short, clear message is enough: "Thanks for today. I enjoyed meeting you, but I don't think we're a fit." You aren't required to give reasons.
- If something went wrong, write down what happened while it's fresh, and keep any messages. You can stop replying to someone who made you uncomfortable.
- For scams or money requests, see [Scamwatch](https://www.scamwatch.gov.au/) and report cybercrime through [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/). For online harassment or images shared without consent, the [eSafety Commissioner](https://www.esafety.gov.au/) can help.

If you're in danger at any point, call 000.

## A first date safety checklist to keep on your phone

| When | Check |
|---|---|
| Before | Public place, friend told, phone charged, transport sorted |
| During | Own drink, own transport, stay public, check in |
| Leaving | A clear sentence ready, no reason owed |
| After | Tell your friend you're home, note how you felt |
