The best gym photo lighting is soft light from the side or front, like a window, with the bright source on your face and not behind you. Avoid standing directly under ceiling lights, and tap your phone screen to set focus and exposure on your face. A few test shots will tell you more than any setting.

## Why gym lighting is tricky

Gyms are built for training, not for photos. Most use bright, hard lights set in rows across the ceiling. Add big mirrors, and you get glare, reflections and odd shadows.

3 problems show up most often:

- **Shadows on your face.** Light from straight above darkens the eyes and under the brow.
- **Silhouettes.** A bright window behind you makes your face dark.
- **Mixed colours.** Ceiling lights and daylight can differ, and skin tone turns yellow or blue.

All 3 are easy to fix once you know what to look for. And none of them needs more than the phone in your pocket.

## Use windows, but face them

Daylight is the cheapest good light you have. Stand so that the window is in front of you or to one side, not behind your head.

Here's a simple way to check. Hold your phone up and look at your face on the screen. If you can clearly see your eyes and both sides of your face, the light is fine. If your face is darker than the background, turn around.

For a mirror selfie, put the window beside the mirror, so the light falls on you and not on the glass. A window directly behind your phone can reflect as a bright patch. [How to take a good mirror selfie for your profile](/blog/mirror-selfie-tips-for-your-profile/) covers the rest of the setup.

## How to dodge overhead glare

If there's no window, you have to work with the ceiling.

1. Step 1 to 2 metres away from the spot directly under a light. Light that hits from the side or front is kinder than light from straight above.
2. Tilt your chin down a little and look slightly toward the light.
3. Try a spot near a pale wall. It bounces light back onto you.
4. Check the mirror for bright patches where a light reflects. Move a step until the patch no longer sits on your face.

Dark corners aren't the answer either. A dim photo gets grainy and soft, so aim for even and bright, not moody.

## Phone settings that help

You don't need to change much. These few steps do most of the work.

- **Clean the lens.** A smudge makes light spread into a haze.
- **Tap your face on the screen.** This sets focus and exposure there. If your phone shows a brightness slider, drag it a little until your face looks natural.
- **Turn the flash off.** It flattens your face, and in a mirror it bounces straight back.
- **Skip digital zoom.** Step closer instead, because zoom makes images blurry.
- **Turn on the grid lines.** They help you keep walls and floors level.
- **Take 5 or more shots.** Pick the best and delete the rest.

If your phone has a night mode, leave it off for gym shots, since it can make movement blurry.

## Pick the right time of day

If you can choose, shoot in daylight hours. Mid-morning or late afternoon often gives soft window light, and a quiet gym gives you space without anyone in the frame. Early morning or late evening gyms rely on ceiling lights, so they're harder.

A quiet time also helps with a bigger issue. Photos of other people without their consent aren't allowed on SetsOfLove, so an empty corner makes your life simpler.

If your gym is always crowded when it's bright, try outdoors. A shot in the car park or a nearby park, in the shade, often looks better than anything indoors. Shade is the key: bright sun makes squinting and sharp shadows.

## A quick fix table

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dark eyes | Light from straight above | Step aside, chin down, face the light |
| Face in shadow | Window behind you | Turn so the window is in front |
| Yellow or blue skin | Mixed light sources | Move to one light source, mostly daylight |
| Soft, grainy photo | Too dim or smudged lens | Find brighter spot, clean lens |
| White blob on the mirror | Flash or reflection | Flash off, shift a step |

## Last check before you pick a photo

Look at each shot at small size, on your phone. Can you see your eyes? Does your skin look like it does in person? If yes, you've got a keeper.
